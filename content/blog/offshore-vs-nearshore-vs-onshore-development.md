Offshore vs nearshore development is mostly a trade between hourly cost and hours of live overlap. Offshore teams, in a distant region with a big time difference, are usually cheapest per hour; nearshore teams, in a neighboring region, cost more but share most of your working day; onshore teams cost the most and remove distance entirely. None of the three is better in general; the right one depends on how much of your project needs real-time conversation, and how well you can write down what you want.

## What do onshore, nearshore and offshore mean?

Onshore means a team in your own country. Nearshore means a team in a nearby country with similar working hours. Offshore means a team in a distant region, usually with a time difference of five hours or more.

The labels depend on where you sit:

| If you are in | Onshore | Common nearshore options | Common offshore options |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| USA | US firms and freelancers | Mexico, Colombia, Costa Rica, Argentina, Brazil | India, Nepal, the Philippines, Vietnam, Eastern Europe |
| UK or Western Europe | Domestic firms | Poland, Romania, Portugal, the Baltic states | India, Nepal, the Philippines, Latin America |
| Australia | Australian firms | New Zealand, the Philippines, Indonesia | India, Nepal, Eastern Europe, Latin America |
| UAE or Singapore | Local firms | South Asia, including Nepal and India | Europe, the Americas |

Time zone matters more than distance. Nepal is UTC+5:45, so it is only 1 hour 45 minutes ahead of the UAE and 2 hours 15 minutes behind Singapore: effectively nearshore for both, and firmly offshore for a company in California.

## Offshore vs nearshore development: how do they compare?

Offshore wins on hourly cost and talent pool size, nearshore wins on live overlap and travel, and onshore wins on legal familiarity and in-person access. The table below uses rough typical market ranges in USD for experienced agency developers; individual firms fall well outside them.

| Factor | Onshore | Nearshore | Offshore |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Typical agency rate (US or UK client) | Roughly $100-$250 an hour | Roughly $45-$100 | Roughly $25-$75 |
| Live overlap with your team | Full working day | Most of the day | From none to about four hours |
| Visiting for workshops | Easy | A short flight | Long-haul, so rare |
| Contract and IP enforcement | Your own courts | Foreign law, often a familiar legal culture | Foreign law; rely on the contract and on owning your repositories |
| Size of hiring pool | Smallest, most expensive | Medium | Largest |
| Best for | Discovery-heavy, regulated or on-site work | Agile teams that need daily live collaboration | Well-specified builds, long-running teams, overnight progress |

The rate column is the one everyone reads first and the least reliable guide to what you will spend. A cheap team working from a vague brief can cost more in the end than an expensive one working from a clear one.

## What does offshore software development really cost?

The real cost is the rate plus the time your own people spend making the arrangement work. Offshore software development saves money when that overhead stays small, which mostly depends on your side.

Budget for these alongside the invoice:

- **Product ownership.** Someone on your team who answers questions, prioritizes the backlog and reviews work every week. Without that person, no model works, and offshore fails fastest.
- **Onboarding.** Access, environments, documentation and a first few weeks at lower productivity while the team learns your domain.
- **Rework.** Requirements that were obvious to you but never written down. Clear acceptance criteria are cheaper than rework.
- **Meetings at awkward hours.** Someone joins an early or late call each week; rotate it so it is not always the same person.
- **Legal and data paperwork.** Transfer agreements and data processing terms, covered below.
- **Currency and payment fees** on international transfers.

Nearshore teams reduce the meeting and clarification costs, which is why they can come out cheaper overall for projects with lots of open questions. Offshore teams pull ahead on stable, well-understood work where the overlap can be short and the rest of the day productive.

## When is onshore still the right choice?

Onshore is right when the work genuinely needs people in the same room or under the same law. In those cases, paying more per hour is cheaper than forcing a distant model to fit.

- **Fuzzy discovery.** Early product work where nobody can yet write down what "done" looks like, and the answer will come from whiteboard sessions.
- **Strict data residency or clearances.** Some government, defense and health contracts require that data, or the people touching it, stay in the country.
- **On-site systems.** Hardware, factory floors, point-of-sale rollouts and anything that needs hands on equipment.
- **No product owner.** If nobody on your side can review work weekly, a nearby team you can meet in person has a better chance of filling the gap.

## When does nearshore beat offshore, and when is it the other way round?

Nearshore outsourcing beats offshore when the team needs many hours of live contact each day: pair programming with your engineers, frequent design reviews, or incident response during your business hours without an on-call rota. It also suits companies whose culture runs on meetings rather than documents.

Offshore beats nearshore when the scope is well defined, the team is long-running and stable, and your organization is comfortable working in writing. The time difference can become an advantage: feedback sent at the end of your day is often worked through overnight and waiting on a staging site in the morning. Many companies run a hybrid, with an onshore product lead and designer and an offshore or nearshore build team. ## Which legal and data questions apply to every model?

Four questions apply whether the team is down the road or across the world, but distance raises the stakes.

1. **Who owns the work?** The contract should assign intellectual property to you on payment, and code should live in repositories your organization owns from the first commit.
2. **Which law governs the contract?** Choose one, name the dispute process, and make sure termination leaves you with everything needed to continue.
3. **Can personal data leave the country?** Nepal has no EU or UK adequacy decision, so EU and UK clients sending personal data there need standard contractual clauses or the UK's transfer agreement. Australian clients should consider Australian Privacy Principle 8 on cross-border disclosure, and Quebec's Law 25 expects a privacy impact assessment first.
4. **Does the team need live data at all?** Often it does not. Staging environments with anonymized data remove most of the transfer problem.

## Five questions to pick the right model

1. How many hours a day of live conversation does this project really need?
2. Can you describe what done looks like for the first milestone, in writing?
3. Who on your side will review working software every week?
4. Can the data leave your country, and under which legal mechanism?
5. What is your year-one budget, including your own team's time?

Mostly "few hours", "yes", "a named person", "yes" and "tight" points offshore. Mostly "all day" and "not yet" points onshore or nearshore. Our guide on [how to hire a remote web developer](/blog/how-to-hire-a-remote-web-developer) covers vetting once you have chosen.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is nearshore development more expensive than offshore?

Usually, per hour. Nearshore rates for US and UK clients commonly sit above offshore rates from South and Southeast Asia. Total cost is closer than the rates suggest, because nearshore teams need fewer late calls and less written clarification. For well-specified, long-running work offshore is normally cheaper overall; for projects with many open questions the gap can narrow or disappear.

### What is the difference between offshoring and outsourcing?

Outsourcing means another company does the work. Offshoring means the work happens in a distant country. They often go together, but not always: you can outsource to a firm in your own city, or offshore by opening your own subsidiary abroad. When people say "offshore development" they usually mean outsourcing to a firm in a distant time zone.

### Is offshore development risky for intellectual property?

The risk is manageable with ordinary controls. Use a contract that assigns IP to you on payment, keep repositories in your own organization from day one, give the team individual accounts you can revoke, and avoid sharing production data where staging data will do. These protections matter just as much onshore; distance only makes enforcing a bad contract harder.

### Can an offshore team work in my time zone?

Some will shift hours, and a partial shift of two or three hours is common and sustainable. A full shift, with a team in Nepal or India working through the US night every day, tends to hurt retention and quality over time. A better design is a fixed daily overlap window plus written handovers, so both sides keep normal working hours.
