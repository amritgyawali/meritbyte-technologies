Website design cost in Canada in 2026 typically falls between C$2,000 and C$7,000 when a freelancer builds a small business site, and C$7,000 to C$25,000 when a small agency does, before GST/HST. Online stores, bilingual English-French builds and custom web apps cost more. These are typical market ranges that vary by province, scope and supplier, and Canada has a handful of rules that push quotes up in ways buyers do not always expect.

## How much does website design cost in Canada?

Start with what the site has to do, because that fixes the band. Supplier choice and Canadian requirements then decide where in the band you end up. All figures below are typical market ranges before sales tax, not quotes.

| Project | Typical range (CAD, before tax) | Usually built by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Template site on a builder (Wix, Squarespace, Shopify starter) | Monthly subscription plus your own time | You, or a freelancer for a few hours of setup |
| Small business site, 5-12 pages, English only | C$2,000-C$7,000 (freelancer), C$7,000-C$25,000 (agency) | Freelancer or small studio |
| Same site in English and French | Add professional translation plus extra build and QA time | Studio with translation partners |
| Shopify or WooCommerce store | C$5,000-C$15,000 (freelancer), C$15,000-C$60,000+ (agency) | E-commerce specialist or agency |
| Custom web app, portal or SaaS product | C$60,000-C$250,000+ | Agency or software firm |

Toronto and Vancouver agencies generally price at the top of these bands. Studios in smaller cities, and offshore teams, often come in lower for comparable scope.

## What are typical web developer rates in Canada?

Canadian freelance web developers commonly charge about C$50 to C$130 an hour, and agencies about C$100 to C$200 an hour. Offshore agencies commonly quote C$35 to C$90 an hour. Seniority and specialism move all three.

| Supplier | Typical hourly rate (CAD) | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Freelancer | C$50-C$130 | Whether design, copy and testing are included or yours to arrange |
| Canadian agency | C$100-C$200 | Minimum project sizes; account management hours billed separately |
| Offshore agency | C$35-C$90 | Overlap hours, who manages the project, where data is accessed from |

Compare on hours per phase as well as rate. A quote that is cheap per hour but vague about hours is not a cheap quote.

## Where does the money go in a small business website?

Most of a small business website cost in Canada goes on design and development, but content and translation are the lines that most often surprise people. A typical split for a small agency project looks roughly like this, though every project differs:

| Phase | Rough share of budget | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery and planning | 10-15% | Sitemap, page goals, content inventory, technical choices |
| Design | 20-25% | Wireframes, visual design, mobile layouts, accessibility of colors and type |
| Development | 35-45% | Templates, CMS setup, forms, integrations, performance |
| Content and translation | 10-20% | Copywriting, French translation, image selection, alt text |
| QA and accessibility testing | About 10% | Browsers, phones, keyboard and screen reader checks |
| Launch and training | About 5% | Redirects, analytics, handover session |

If a quote shows almost nothing for content or testing, those jobs have not disappeared. They have been handed back to you.

## Which Canadian rules change the quote?

Five things make a Canadian website different from a generic one. A good proposal names each of them; a cheap one usually leaves them out.

### French-language requirements

Quebec's Charter of the French Language, strengthened by Bill 96 in 2022, requires a business's commercial content aimed at Quebec, including its website, to be available in French on terms at least as favorable as any other language. Online terms that consumers accept, such as checkout conditions, generally have to be presented in French first. Budget for professional translation rather than machine output, a bilingual content structure with hreflang tags, and a second round of QA.

### CASL consent on every form

Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation has applied to commercial email since 2014, with penalties of up to C$10 million per violation for businesses. On the website that means unchecked consent boxes, clear wording about what people are signing up for, a record of when and where consent was given, and an unsubscribe that is processed within 10 business days. Our guide to [email marketing for small businesses](/blog/email-marketing-for-small-business) covers consent and deliverability in more detail.

### Privacy: PIPEDA, Law 25 and provincial laws

PIPEDA is still the federal private-sector privacy law; the proposed replacement in Bill C-27 died when Parliament was prorogued in January 2025. Alberta and British Columbia have their own PIPA laws. Quebec's Law 25, fully phased in by 2024, is the strictest: it expects a named person in charge of privacy, tracking technologies that can identify or profile people switched off by default, and a privacy impact assessment before personal information is communicated outside Quebec.

That last point matters if an overseas team will be able to see customer data. Keep developers on staging data where you can, and document the assessment where you cannot.

### AODA and accessibility

In Ontario, private and non-profit organizations with 50 or more employees must make their public websites meet WCAG 2.0 level AA under the AODA's Integrated Accessibility Standards, with narrow exceptions for live captions and pre-recorded audio description. Federally regulated businesses have obligations under the Accessible Canada Act. Building to WCAG 2.2 AA covers the older standard and is the sensible default everywhere.

### GST, HST and PST on the invoice

The sales tax a Canadian supplier charges generally depends on where your business is, not where the supplier is. That means 5% GST in Alberta and the territories; 13% HST in Ontario; 15% HST in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador and Prince Edward Island; 14% in Nova Scotia since April 2025; GST plus 9.975% QST in Quebec; and GST plus PST or RST in British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba, where whether PST applies depends on what is being supplied. Suppliers under the C$30,000 small supplier threshold may not charge GST/HST at all. An overseas developer invoicing a registered Canadian business usually does not charge GST/HST, though businesses not fully entitled to input tax credits may need to self-assess, so check with your accountant.

## Local agency, freelancer or offshore team?

Choose by what the project needs, then by price. If the site must be bilingual, pass AODA review and satisfy a legal team, a Canadian agency with translation partners may be worth its rate. If it is a small English-only site with a clear brief, a local freelancer is often the best value.

An offshore team makes sense for larger builds, e-commerce and ongoing retainers, where your team is comfortable reviewing work in writing and does not need in-person workshops. It also works well alongside a Canadian translator or accessibility auditor you hire directly. The questions to settle before signing are the same everywhere: who owns the code and accounts, how often you will see working software, and where your customers' data will be accessed from. ## Frequently asked questions

### What should a Canadian small business budget for a website?

For a 5-12 page English-only site, budget roughly C$2,000 to C$7,000 with a freelancer or C$7,000 to C$25,000 with a small agency, plus GST/HST. Treat those as typical market ranges rather than quotes; custom design, content, integrations and accessibility work all move the number. Adding French means translation and a second QA pass. Keep a separate yearly line for hosting, domain renewal and maintenance.

### Does my website need to be in French?

If your business operates in Quebec or aims its commercial content at Quebec customers, the Charter of the French Language generally requires a French version at least as available as any other language. Outside Quebec, French is usually a business choice, though federal institutions have official languages obligations. Selling nationally to consumers is a good reason to offer French regardless of the legal minimum.

### What does CASL mean for my website's forms?

Any form that adds people to marketing emails needs an unchecked consent checkbox, plain wording about what they will receive, your business identification, and a stored record of the consent. Every commercial email must include a working unsubscribe, processed within 10 business days. Contact forms that only answer the inquiry do not need marketing consent, but should not quietly add people to a newsletter.

### Is it cheaper to hire an offshore web developer from Canada?

Usually, if the scope is written down. Offshore agency rates are commonly a fraction of Canadian agency rates, but you spend more of your own time on written feedback and scheduled calls. For bilingual or AODA-bound projects, pairing an offshore build team with a Canadian translator or accessibility auditor often gives the best balance of cost and assurance.
