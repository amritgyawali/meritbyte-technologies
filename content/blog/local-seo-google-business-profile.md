Google Business Profile optimization is the fastest local SEO work most businesses can do: verify the profile properly, choose the right primary category, fill in every field that applies and build a steady flow of genuine reviews. Google says it ranks local results on three things, relevance, distance and prominence. You control relevance and prominence more than you might think. Distance you cannot change, and nobody should try to fake it.

This guide goes step by step, then ends with a checklist you can work through in an afternoon.

## How does Google decide who ranks in Google Maps?

Google's own help pages name three factors for local results:

- **Relevance**: how well your profile matches what the person searched for. Categories, services, the description and your website's content all feed it.
- **Distance**: how far your business, or the area you serve, is from the searcher or from the place named in the search.
- **Prominence**: how well known the business is, including review count and rating, links and articles about you across the web, directory listings and your website's position in ordinary search results.

"Near me" searches work through the searcher's location, not through the words on your page. You do not need to write "near me" anywhere; you need the service and the place stated clearly, and a profile Google trusts.

## Step 1: Set up and verify the profile the right way

Google Business Profile is for businesses that meet customers in person, either at a location customers visit or by traveling to them.

**Choose the right type.** If customers come to you (a clinic, a shop, a restaurant), show your address. If you go to customers (a plumber, a cleaner, a mobile mechanic) and do not serve people at your premises, set it up as a service-area business: hide the address and list up to 20 service areas. Google still needs a real address to verify you; it just does not show it.

**Use a real, eligible address.** PO boxes and mail drops are not eligible, and virtual offices are only allowed if they are staffed during your business hours. Do not rent a mailbox in a suburb you want to rank in, and do not create a profile for every area you serve. Profiles built on fake addresses get suspended, and they send customers to places you are not.

**Use your real business name**, exactly as it appears on your signage, registration and documents. Adding keywords ("Smith Plumbing, Emergency Plumber Sydney") breaks Google's guidelines. Competitors can report it through "Suggest an edit" or the Business Redressal Complaint Form, and the result can be a suspension.

**Verify.** Google decides which methods you are offered, and they can include a video recording of your premises and proof of management, a phone call or text, email, a live video call or a postcard. Record the video in one continuous take showing the exterior, signage or equipment, and proof that you manage the business.

## Step 2: Choose your categories carefully

The primary category tells Google what your business is and decides which searches you are considered for. Pick the most specific one that fits: "Italian restaurant" rather than "Restaurant", "Family law attorney" rather than "Lawyer".

You can add up to nine additional categories. Use them only for services you genuinely offer as a core part of the business. Look at the categories of competitors who appear for your main searches; you can see them on their profiles in Google Maps.

## Step 3: Fill in every field that is true

| Field | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Hours | Regular hours, plus special hours for public holidays and festivals so customers do not arrive at a closed door |
| Phone | A number a person answers during business hours |
| Website | Link to the most relevant page (the location page for multi-location businesses) with UTM tags, such as `utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp` |
| Description | Up to 750 characters: what you do, for whom and where; no links, prices or promotions |
| Services and products | List each with a short description, and prices where you can publish them |
| Attributes | Accessibility, payment options, amenities and others offered for your category |
| Booking or order links | Your own booking page, if you have one |
| Photos | Real photos of the exterior, interior, team and work; replace stock images |
| Updates (posts) | Offers, events and news; useful for customers, with no confirmed ranking effect |

Two features have gone: chat and messaging in Business Profiles ended in July 2024, and the free business.site websites Google offered were shut down the same year. If your enquiry process relied on either, move it to your website, WhatsApp or phone.

## Step 4: Build a steady flow of genuine reviews

Reviews are a large part of prominence and of whether anyone clicks on you. The goal is a steady flow of real reviews, not a burst.

1. **Ask every customer**, at the moment the job is done. Google's policies prohibit review gating, which means asking only happy customers, and prohibit offering incentives for reviews.
2. **Make it easy.** Use the review link from your Business Profile dashboard in a follow-up text or email, and print it as a QR code for the counter or invoice.
3. **Reply to every review**, positive and negative, briefly and factually. Never confirm private details: a clinic should not confirm that someone was a patient.
4. **Never buy reviews or post fake ones.** In the US, the Federal Trade Commission's rule banning fake reviews took effect in October 2024; in the UK, the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 banned fake reviews from April 2025.
5. **Keep other platforms healthy** where your customers look: Tripadvisor for tourism, Facebook, Yelp in North America, or the review sites for your trade.

## Step 5: Make your website agree with your profile

Your profile and website should reinforce each other.

- **Name, address and phone** on the website should match the profile exactly, down to the suite number format.
- **LocalBusiness structured data** on the home or contact page should repeat the same details, hours and `sameAs` links. Our guide to [schema markup for small businesses](/blog/schema-markup-for-small-business) lists the properties.
- **One page per real location**, with directions, parking, opening hours, staff and services specific to that place.
- **Service-area pages** only when you can say something specific about each area. Near-identical pages with the suburb name swapped are doorway pages under Google's spam policies.
- **Title tags** that include the service and the place, such as "Emergency dentist in Parramatta".
- **Consistent listings elsewhere**: Apple Business Connect, Bing Places for Business, Facebook and the main directories in your industry. Consistency matters more than volume.

## Step 6: Measure what Google Business Profile optimization produces

The Performance section of your Business Profile shows the search terms people used, views, calls, direction requests, website clicks and bookings. The UTM tags from Step 3 separate profile traffic from other organic visits in GA4.

Map rankings vary street by street, because distance changes with every searcher. A single "we rank third" figure means little. Grid-based rank trackers such as Local Falcon or BrightLocal show how visibility changes across your area, which is more useful for spotting where you are weak.

## The local SEO checklist

| Check | Done when |
| --- | --- |
| Eligibility | Real, staffed address or a service-area setup; no virtual mailboxes |
| Name | Matches signage and registration, with no added keywords |
| Primary category | The most specific accurate option |
| Profile fields | Hours, phone, website with UTM tags, description, services, attributes |
| Photos | Real, recent and covering the exterior, interior and work |
| Reviews | Every customer asked; every review answered; nothing bought or gated |
| Website | Same name, address and phone; LocalBusiness schema; a page per location |
| Listings | Apple, Bing, Facebook and industry directories consistent |
| Tracking | Profile performance reviewed monthly; UTM traffic visible in GA4 |

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I rank in Google Maps without a physical address?

You need a real address to verify, but you do not have to show it. Businesses that travel to customers can set up as a service-area business, hide the address and list up to 20 service areas. Home-based businesses often work this way. What you cannot do is use a virtual mailbox or an address you do not occupy.

### How long do Google Business Profile changes take to affect rankings?

Edits to categories, hours and details usually go live within days once Google accepts them, though some are held for review. The ranking effect of new categories or better fields can appear within a few weeks. Reviews and wider prominence build over months, so judge progress quarterly using calls, direction requests and leads.

### Should I put keywords in my Google Business Profile name?

No. Google's guidelines require the name you use in the real world, on signage and documents. Adding services or place names can trigger a suspension, and competitors can report it. If your registered name already includes a service word, that is fine. Put keywords in your categories, services, description and website instead.

### How do I get more Google reviews without breaking the rules?

Ask every customer, not only the happy ones, at the moment the job is finished, using the short review link from your profile in a text, an email or a QR code. Do not offer discounts or gifts for reviews, and do not filter who gets asked. Replying to existing reviews also shows future customers that someone is listening.
