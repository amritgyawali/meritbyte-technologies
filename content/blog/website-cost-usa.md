How much does a website cost in the USA? In 2026 a small business site typically runs about $2,000 to $8,000 with a freelancer and $8,000 to $30,000 with a small agency, while online stores and custom web apps often cost $15,000 to well over $100,000. These are typical market ranges that vary with scope, region and who does the work. Below is what sits behind those numbers, including the US-specific costs that cheap quotes tend to leave out.

## How much does a website cost in the USA for each stage of business?

Price follows the job the site has to do. A five-page site that proves you exist costs a fraction of one that books appointments, syncs with a CRM and passes an accessibility audit.

| Stage of business | What the site usually includes | Typical total (USD) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Just starting | DIY builder such as Squarespace, Wix or Shopify Starter, a template, your own copy | Tens of dollars a month, plus your time |
| Established local business | 5-12 pages on WordPress or similar, custom design, contact forms, Google Business Profile setup | $2,000-$8,000 (freelancer), $8,000-$30,000 (agency) |
| Growing service company | Booking, CRM integration, blog, landing pages for ads, accessibility testing | $10,000-$50,000 |
| Online store | Shopify or WooCommerce, product import, shipping rules, sales tax setup, email flows | $5,000-$15,000 (freelancer), $15,000-$75,000+ (agency) |
| Custom platform | Customer portal, SaaS product or internal web app with logins and data | $50,000-$250,000+ |

These ranges cover the build only. Hosting, plugins, apps, maintenance and content changes come on top, every year.

## What is the typical web developer hourly rate in the USA?

US freelance web developers commonly charge $50 to $150 an hour, and US agencies $100 to $250 an hour. Offshore agencies commonly quote $25 to $75. All three move with seniority, specialism and location.

Region still matters, even for remote work. Agencies in New York, San Francisco, Boston and Los Angeles typically sit at the top of the range, and studios in much of the Midwest and South price lower for comparable work. Specialists in accessibility remediation, headless builds or complex Shopify Plus stores charge premiums wherever they are based.

Hourly rate is the least useful number on a quote, though. A $150-an-hour developer who needs 60 hours can cost less than a $60-an-hour one who needs 200.

## A worked example: one site priced three ways

Take a 15-page site for a service business: custom design, online booking through a tool such as Calendly or Acuity, forms that feed HubSpot, a blog, redirects from the old site and WCAG 2.2 AA testing. A realistic estimate is 120 to 180 hours of work, depending on revisions and how ready the content is.

| Supplier | Illustrative rate | 120 hours | 180 hours | What else you pay in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| US agency | $150/hour | $18,000 | $27,000 | Little extra time; project management is included |

The rates are illustrations inside the typical ranges, not quotes. The point is the shape: the offshore line saves real money only if the brief is clear and someone on your side reviews work every week. ## Which US-specific costs do cheap quotes leave out?

Three things set US projects apart: accessibility litigation, a patchwork of state privacy laws, and state sales tax. Each needs real hours, and none is optional for a business that sells online.

### ADA accessibility

Title III of the Americans with Disabilities Act covers businesses open to the public, and the Department of Justice's 2022 guidance confirms it treats their websites as covered. There is no technical standard written into the regulation for private businesses, but WCAG 2.1 or 2.2 level AA is what lawsuits and settlements measure against. Website accessibility claims are filed in large numbers, heavily concentrated in New York and California; California's Unruh Civil Rights Act adds statutory damages of at least $4,000 per violation.

Accessibility overlay widgets are not a shortcut. In 2025 the Federal Trade Commission took action against the overlay vendor accessiBe over claims that its tool made websites compliant. Budget instead for accessible templates, keyboard and screen reader testing, captions and alt text, and a re-test after major changes.

### State privacy laws

The California Consumer Privacy Act, as amended by the CPRA, applies to for-profit businesses that meet thresholds such as an inflation-adjusted annual revenue figure that started at $25 million, or buying, selling or sharing the personal information of 100,000 or more California consumers or households. A growing list of states, including Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut and Texas, have their own comprehensive laws.

On the website this means a privacy policy that matches what the site actually collects, a "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link where required, honoring Global Privacy Control signals, and a consent tool that controls which third-party scripts load. Lawsuits over tracking pixels and chat widgets under older wiretap and video privacy laws have made careful tag management part of the build, not an afterthought.

### Sales tax

Whether web design and development services are taxable depends on the state, and hosting, SaaS and software are often treated differently from design labor. Ask your accountant how your state treats the invoice.

For online stores the bigger cost is collecting tax. Since the Supreme Court's 2018 *South Dakota v. Wayfair* decision, states can require remote sellers to collect sales tax once they pass an economic nexus threshold, commonly $100,000 in sales into that state. A store selling nationally needs a tax engine such as Shopify Tax, Stripe Tax or Avalara, correct product tax codes, and a process for exemption certificates if you sell to businesses.

## Local agency, freelancer or offshore: which fits a US business?

Pick by risk, not rate. A local agency fits when stakeholders need workshops and the launch date is fixed; a freelancer fits a well-defined small site; an offshore team fits ongoing or larger builds where you can manage by writing.

- **Choose a US agency** if several departments must sign off, you need in-person brand workshops, or you operate in a regulated field and want a domestic contract and insurance.
- **Choose a US freelancer** for a brochure site or a small Shopify build with a clear scope, when you are comfortable coordinating copy and photography yourself. Expect to collect a W-9 and, above the IRS reporting threshold, file a 1099-NEC unless the freelancer's business is a corporation; confirm the current threshold with your accountant.
- **Choose an offshore agency** for larger builds, retainers or white-label overflow, when you can give written feedback on a staging site and accept an overlap window instead of all-day availability. A foreign contractor typically gives you a W-8BEN instead of a W-9.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the average cost of a small business website in the USA?

There is no reliable single average, because the range is so wide. For a typical 5-12 page small business site, freelancers commonly charge about $2,000 to $8,000 and small agencies about $8,000 to $30,000 in 2026. Custom design, copywriting, integrations and accessibility testing push the price up. Budget separately for hosting, maintenance and content updates each year.

### Do I pay sales tax on web design services in the USA?

It depends on your state. Many states do not tax web design labor, while some tax certain digital services, hosting or software. The answer can also change if the invoice bundles design with a software license or hosting. Ask your accountant, and ask your developer to itemize services, hosting and software separately so the invoice can be taxed correctly.

### Will an accessibility overlay widget protect me from ADA lawsuits?

No. Overlay widgets do not fix the underlying code, and websites using them have still been sued. The Federal Trade Commission acted against the overlay vendor accessiBe in 2025 over its compliance claims. The reliable route is building and testing to WCAG 2.1 or 2.2 level AA, publishing an accessibility statement with a contact route, and re-testing after significant changes.

### Is offshore web development really cheaper for US businesses?

Usually, once the brief is clear. An offshore agency's hourly rate is commonly a fraction of a US agency's, but you pay some of the difference in your own time: writing tickets, reviewing staging builds and joining an early or late call each week. For well-scoped builds and ongoing retainers the saving is real; for vague projects it often disappears.
