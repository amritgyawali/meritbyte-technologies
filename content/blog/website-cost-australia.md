Website cost in Australia in 2026 usually lands between A$1,500 and A$5,000 for a small business site built by a freelancer, A$5,000 to A$15,000 from a small local agency, and A$15,000 to well past A$50,000 for e-commerce, booking systems or custom web apps. Those are typical market ranges before GST, and they vary with scope, city and who does the work. The rest of this guide explains what moves a quote within those bands, and when a local agency, a freelancer or an offshore team is the better buy.

## How much does a website cost in Australia by project type?

The type of site sets the range; the supplier sets where you land in it. The figures below are typical market ranges excluding GST, gathered from how Australian freelancers and agencies commonly price work. Individual quotes will fall outside them.

| Project | Freelancer | Small local agency | What drives it up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DIY builder (Wix, Squarespace, Shopify starter) | Tens of dollars a month in subscriptions, plus your time | Not usually offered | Paid apps, premium templates, your hours |
| Small business site, 5-10 pages, WordPress or similar | A$1,500-A$5,000 | A$5,000-A$15,000 | Custom design, copywriting, photography |
| Lead-generation site with booking, quoting or CRM integration | A$5,000-A$12,000 | A$12,000-A$35,000 | Number of integrations, forms and user roles |
| Shopify or WooCommerce store | A$4,000-A$12,000 | A$10,000-A$40,000 | Catalogue size, migrations, shipping and ERP links |
| Custom web app or customer portal | Rarely a solo job | A$40,000-A$150,000+ | Logins, permissions, data, security testing |

Sydney and Melbourne agencies tend to sit at the upper end; regional studios often price lower for similar work. The cheapest quotes usually leave out copywriting, accessibility testing and redirects from your old site, which is where most post-launch surprises come from.

## What do web developers charge per hour in Australia?

Australian freelance web developers commonly charge about A$60 to A$150 an hour, and agencies about A$120 to A$220 an hour, excluding GST. Offshore agencies typically charge a fraction of the local agency rate. All three vary widely with seniority and specialism.

| Supplier | Typical rate (ex GST) | What the rate usually includes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local freelancer | A$60-A$150 per hour | One person's time; design, build or both |
| Local agency | A$120-A$220 per hour, often sold as A$900-A$1,700 per day | Project management, design, development, QA, account management |
| Offshore agency | Commonly A$30-A$90 per hour | Development and QA, sometimes design; project management varies |

A lower hourly rate only saves money if the hours stay under control. Ask each supplier for an estimate in hours per phase, not just a total, so you can compare like with like. Our guide to [comparing web development quotes](/blog/how-to-compare-web-development-quotes) has a checklist for that.

## Is a website quote in Australia inc or ex GST?

Business-to-business quotes are usually ex GST, and many small freelancers are not registered for GST at all, so two quotes can differ by 10% before anyone has compared scope. Ask every supplier to state it on the quote.

The rules worth knowing:

- **Registration.** A supplier must register for GST once its GST turnover reaches A$75,000. Below that, a freelancer's invoice may carry no GST, which only helps you if you are not registered yourself.
- **Your own prices.** If you sell to consumers, the Australian Consumer Law requires advertised prices to show the total, including GST. An online store must display GST-inclusive prices and, for business buyers, issue tax invoices; a tax invoice is required on request for sales above A$82.50 including GST.
- **Local freelancers and ABNs.** A local freelancer should quote an ABN on invoices. If they do not, you are generally required to withhold tax at the top marginal rate from the payment.
- **Offshore suppliers.** An overseas developer invoicing a GST-registered Australian business generally does not add GST. Check the treatment for your circumstances with your accountant.

## What Australian compliance work adds to the cost?

Four pieces of Australian law and one registry rule shape the scope of a business website. Good quotes include them explicitly; cheap ones tend to leave them out.

1. **Accessibility.** The Disability Discrimination Act 1992 applies to websites, and the Human Rights and Equal Opportunity Commission's decision in Maguire v SOCOG (2000), over the Sydney Olympics website, made that clear a long time ago. WCAG 2.2 level AA is the sensible target; testing with a keyboard and a screen reader adds days, not hours.
2. **Privacy.** The Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles apply to businesses with annual turnover above A$3 million, and to some smaller ones, such as health service providers. Reforms passed in 2024 are phasing in, including privacy-policy disclosures about certain automated decisions from December 2026.
3. **Email marketing.** The Spam Act 2003 requires consent, clear sender details and an unsubscribe that works within five working days. That affects how sign-up forms and your email platform are configured.
4. **Payments and checkout.** Stripe, PayPal, Afterpay, Zip, Apple Pay and Google Pay are all common; each one added is more checkout testing. Shipping integrations with Australia Post, StarTrack or Sendle add their own work.
5. **Domains.** A .com.au licence requires an Australian presence, typically an ABN or ACN. An overseas developer without an Australian presence cannot hold one, and should not hold your domain anyway: it belongs in your name.

Data location can matter too. AWS, Azure and Google Cloud all run Sydney and Melbourne regions, and some clients in health, finance or government supply chains ask for hosting there.

## Agency, freelancer or offshore: which suits an Australian business?

A local freelancer suits a small, well-defined site; a local agency suits work that needs several specialists and face-to-face workshops; an offshore team suits ongoing or larger builds where you can write things down and keep a weekly rhythm.

| Factor | Local agency | Local freelancer | Offshore team |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Typical cost | Highest | Low to medium | Low to medium |
| Same working hours | Yes | Yes | Partial overlap |
| In-person workshops | Yes | Sometimes | No |
| Several specialists | Yes | Usually one person, sometimes a network | Usually yes |
| Continuity if someone leaves | Good | Weak | Depends on the firm |
| Best for | Brand-heavy launches, regulated clients, complex stakeholder groups | Brochure sites, small WordPress or Shopify builds | Larger builds, retainers, e-commerce, web apps on a budget |

The offshore column hides the most variation, from excellent to disastrous. The difference is rarely talent; it is process. Look for a named project manager, a weekly written update, a staging site you can open yourself, and code, hosting and domain accounts in your name from day one. ## What ongoing costs should Australian businesses budget for?

Plan for yearly running costs on top of the build. They are smaller, but they never stop.

- **Domain:** a .com.au or .au licence, renewed every one to five years.
- **Hosting:** managed WordPress or cloud hosting, monthly or annually; Shopify plans are monthly, and paid apps add up.
- **Maintenance:** plugin, theme and framework updates, backups that are tested, and uptime monitoring.
- **Small changes:** new pages, seasonal banners, price updates, often sold as a block of hours.
- **Security and accessibility checks** after significant changes.

A useful rule when comparing quotes: ask what the first year costs in total, not just the launch.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a small business website cost in Australia in 2026?

A 5-10 page small business website typically costs A$1,500 to A$5,000 from a freelancer and A$5,000 to A$15,000 from a small local agency, excluding GST. These are typical market ranges that vary with design, copywriting, integrations and accessibility testing. Add yearly costs for the domain, hosting and maintenance. Ask each supplier to break the quote into phases so you can compare like with like.

### Should an Australian website quote include GST?

Ask for it to be stated either way. B2B quotes are usually ex GST, and freelancers below the A$75,000 registration threshold may not charge it at all. If your business is registered for GST you can generally claim it back, so the ex-GST figure is the fair comparison. If you are not registered, compare GST-inclusive totals, because that is what you will pay.

### Is it safe to hire an offshore web developer as an Australian business?

It can be, if the setup protects you. Keep the domain, hosting, code repository and any payment accounts in your business's name, pay by milestone rather than all upfront, and insist on a staging site you can check whenever you like. Agree the working-hours overlap in writing. Most failed offshore projects fail on vague briefs and missing oversight, not on skill.

### Does my website legally need to be accessible in Australia?

The Disability Discrimination Act 1992 makes it unlawful to discriminate against people with disability in providing goods and services, and that has been applied to websites. There is no WCAG certificate you must hold, but WCAG 2.2 level AA is the accepted benchmark. Building to it from the start is far cheaper than fixing a finished site after a complaint.
