Website cost in the UK in 2026 typically runs from about £1,000 to £5,000 if you hire a freelancer for a small business site, £4,000 to £15,000 with a small agency, and £10,000 to £50,000 or more for e-commerce and bespoke builds. These are typical market ranges, not fixed prices; they vary with scope, location, VAT and who does the work. What follows is what each budget band actually buys, and the UK-specific items that turn a cheap quote into an expensive launch.

## How much does a website cost in the UK? What each budget band buys

Think in bands rather than averages. Each one buys a different kind of site, and moving up a band usually means paying for thinking, not just more pages.

| Budget | What you typically get | Usually from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under £1,000 | A Squarespace, Wix or Shopify template; you write the copy and supply the photos | You, or a freelancer for a day or two of setup |
| £1,000-£5,000 | 5-10 page WordPress or builder site, adapted template design, contact forms, basic on-page SEO | Freelancer or very small studio |
| £5,000-£15,000 | Custom design, help with copy, booking or CRM integration, accessibility checks, redirects from the old site | Small agency or senior freelancer |
| £15,000-£50,000 | Larger site with a design system, e-commerce with stock and shipping integrations, several stakeholders to satisfy | Established agency |
| £50,000+ | Customer portals, web apps, headless or high-volume e-commerce | Agency or software firm |

Web design prices in the UK also track geography: London agencies price well above most regional studios for similar work. The cheapest band is often the right one for a sole trader, and there is no shame in it.

## What is a typical web developer day rate in the UK?

UK freelance web developers commonly charge about £250 to £550 a day, and agencies £450 to £1,000 or more a day. Offshore agencies commonly quote around £150 to £350 a day. These are typical ranges that move with seniority, specialism and city.

| Supplier | Typical day rate | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Freelancer, regional | £250-£450 | Often not VAT-registered; one person's availability |
| Freelancer, London or specialist | £400-£550+ | Shopify Plus, accessibility and headless specialists at the top |
| Agency | £450-£1,000+ | Includes project management and QA; London agencies at the top |
| Offshore agency | Around £150-£350 | Overlap hours and project management vary by firm |

Day rates only mean something next to an estimate of days. Ask each supplier for days per phase, then compare totals.

## Why do two UK website quotes differ by 20%?

Usually because one supplier is VAT-registered and the other is not. VAT at 20% applies once a business's taxable turnover passes the registration threshold, £90,000 since April 2024, so many freelancers charge none while every agency charges it.

Whether that matters depends on you:

- **If you are VAT-registered**, you can normally reclaim the VAT, so compare quotes on the ex-VAT figure.
- **If you are not registered**, the 20% is a real cost, and an unregistered freelancer has a genuine price advantage.
- **If the supplier is overseas**, a VAT-registered UK business generally accounts for the VAT itself under the reverse charge, which usually nets to nothing if you can recover VAT in full. Confirm the treatment with your accountant.

Your own site's prices are a separate question. Prices shown to consumers must include VAT, and since April 2025 the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 has made it unlawful to add unavoidable fees later in checkout. Build the store so the headline price is the price.

## What does a UK business website legally need?

A handful of UK rules apply to almost every business site, and each takes build time. If a quote does not mention them, ask whether they are included.

- **Cookie consent under PECR.** Non-essential cookies, including most analytics and advertising tags, need consent before they are set, and the ICO expects rejecting to be as easy as accepting. The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 relaxes consent for some low-risk uses, such as certain analytics, as its provisions come into force, so build to current ICO guidance and adjust once your developer confirms what has commenced.
- **A UK GDPR privacy notice.** Under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 your privacy notice should match what the site actually collects, and most businesses that process personal data must pay the ICO data protection fee.
- **Company disclosures.** A limited company's website must show its registered name, company number, place of registration and registered office address, plus a VAT number if registered.
- **Consumer rights for online sales.** The Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 require clear pre-contract information and, for most goods, a 14-day cancellation right.
- **Accessibility.** The Equality Act 2010 requires reasonable adjustments for disabled people, and WCAG 2.2 level AA is the benchmark; public sector bodies have specific regulations of their own.
- **International data transfers.** If developers or support staff outside the UK can access personal data, you need a lawful transfer mechanism, typically the ICO's International Data Transfer Agreement or the UK Addendum to the EU standard contractual clauses.

Payments add their own scope: Stripe, PayPal, Klarna, Clearpay and GoCardless for Direct Debit are all common. Selling to EU customers since Brexit means handling EU VAT and customs data too.

## Agency, freelancer or offshore: how do the costs compare?

The build price is only part of it. Compare what the first year costs, including changes, and how exposed you are if one person disappears.

| Factor | UK agency | UK freelancer | Offshore agency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Build price | Highest | Lowest to medium | Low to medium |
| Rate for later changes | Agency day rate, often sold in blocks | Freelancer day rate, subject to availability | Lower day rate, usually in retainer blocks |
| VAT on invoice | Yes | Often no | Usually reverse charge for VAT-registered clients |
| Same hours as you | Yes | Yes | Partial overlap |
| Continuity | Strong | Weak if the freelancer is busy or leaves | Depends on the firm's handover discipline |
| Best for | Complex stakeholders, regulated sectors, brand launches | Sole traders and small, well-defined sites | Larger builds and ongoing work with a clear written brief |

If you are a medium or large company engaging a freelancer through their own limited company, the off-payroll rules (IR35) make you responsible for a status determination; small companies are exempt and the contractor decides. A defined project with its own deliverables looks different to someone working inside your team every day, but take advice rather than assuming. ## Frequently asked questions

### What do UK small businesses typically pay for a website?

Most pay somewhere between £1,000 and £5,000 when a freelancer builds a 5-10 page site, and £4,000 to £15,000 when a small agency does. Those are typical market ranges; custom design, copywriting, integrations, accessibility testing and VAT decide where you land. A sole trader can start on a builder for a monthly fee. Hosting, domain renewal and maintenance come on top every year.

### Does a UK website need a cookie banner?

If it sets non-essential cookies or similar trackers, such as most analytics, advertising pixels and embedded social media, then yes: PECR requires consent before they are set, and rejecting must be as easy as accepting. A site with only strictly necessary cookies does not need consent, though it still needs to explain them. Some rules on low-risk analytics are being relaxed under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025.

### Should I hire a VAT-registered web developer?

It depends on your own VAT status. If your business is VAT-registered, you can normally reclaim the 20%, so a registered and an unregistered supplier cost the same on the ex-VAT figure. If you are not registered, an unregistered freelancer is effectively 20% cheaper at the same day rate. Either way, ask every supplier to state clearly whether VAT is included.

### What is a fair web developer day rate in London?

London freelancers commonly charge towards the top of the UK range, around £400 to £550 or more a day for experienced developers, and London agencies often charge £700 to £1,000 or more. These are typical ranges that vary with specialism and demand. A fair rate is one that comes with a clear estimate of days, a named person doing the work and code you own.
