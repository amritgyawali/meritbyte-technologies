An education consultancy website in Nepal earns its cost when a late-night search such as "masters in Australia after bachelor's in Nepal" becomes a booked counselling session, and when that student is still being tracked months later at visa stage. That takes destination pages that answer real questions, an enquiry form built around what a counsellor needs, and a student enquiry system behind it. The visual design matters less than most consultancies expect; the follow-up matters more.

## What should an education consultancy website in Nepal include?

It needs one page per destination, one page per service, an intake calendar, real counsellor profiles, branch details and an enquiry form that feeds a tracked pipeline. Most other features are optional.

Here is the minimum set, and the mistake that usually undermines each page:

| Page | What it must answer | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Destination (Australia, UK, Canada, USA, Japan and so on) | Levels and courses you process, intakes, English test expectations, a realistic timeline, the document list | Pasting embassy text with no date on it |
| Test preparation (IELTS, PTE, TOEFL, Duolingo English Test, Japanese language) | Batch start dates, class times, fees, mock test schedule | No batch dates, so every student phones to ask |
| Free counselling | How the first session works, how long it takes, what to bring | A 15-field form before anyone has spoken to the student |
| Counsellor profiles | Name, photo, destinations handled, languages, certifications actually held | Stock photos of people who do not work there |
| Branch pages | Address, map, hours, phone, WhatsApp and Viber links | One page listing every branch, which ranks for none of them |
| Fees and services | What is free, what you charge for, what the institution charges | Silence, which students read as hidden charges |

## Which pages turn searches into counselling bookings?

Destination-and-level pages do most of the work, because they match how students phrase searches: "nursing in the UK for Nepali students", "diploma in Canada after +2", "Japanese language school October intake". A page that answers that exact question well will out-convert a generic "Study in Canada" page every time.

Write each one the way a good counsellor runs a first session. Cover the intakes (September and January for most UK universities; February and July for most Australian ones; fall and spring for the USA), typical academic and English requirements, a timeline worked backwards from the intake, and the documents the student will eventually need.

Include the Nepal-side steps that overseas university pages never mention: getting academic transcripts and character certificates, arranging a bank balance and sponsor documents, and the Ministry of Education's No Objection Certificate (NOC), which banks ask for before they transfer tuition fees abroad. These are the questions students otherwise bring to three consultancies before choosing one.

Date every requirement and link to the official source. Destination rules move: Australia raised its student visa financial capacity figure more than once between 2023 and 2024, and a page quoting an old number costs you credibility with exactly the students who have done their homework. A line such as "Checked against the Department of Home Affairs site in September 2026" is worth more than a paragraph of reassurance.

## How should a student enquiry system work?

A student enquiry system is a shared pipeline in which every enquiry, from any channel, becomes a record with an owner, a stage and a next action. Without one, leads live in counsellors' personal WhatsApp accounts and leave when they do.

A workable set of stages looks like this:

1. **New enquiry.** Logged automatically from the website form, and manually from walk-ins, calls, Facebook Messenger, Viber and WhatsApp, always with the source recorded.
2. **Contacted.** A counsellor has replied; the record shows who, when and on which channel.
3. **Counselled.** First session done; destination, level and intake confirmed.
4. **Documents in progress.** A checklist specific to the destination, ticked off as each item arrives.
5. **Applied.** One line per institution, with application dates and fees paid.
6. **Offer received.** Conditional or unconditional, with conditions listed.
7. **Enrolment confirmed.** CoE for Australia, CAS for the UK, I-20 for the USA, letter of acceptance for Canada.
8. **Visa lodged, then decided.** Dates recorded for both, so you learn your real processing times.

Off-the-shelf CRMs such as HubSpot or Zoho CRM can run this pipeline with custom stages, and for a single-branch consultancy that is usually the sensible start. A custom system starts to pay off when you need branch-level permissions, document checklists that change by destination, or commission tracking per partner institution, which general CRMs handle awkwardly.

Whichever you choose, the website form should write straight into it. The report worth reading every Monday is simple: enquiries by source, then how many became counselling sessions, applications and visas, broken down by branch and destination. It shows which Facebook campaign produced enrolled students rather than just phone numbers.

## What makes students trust a study abroad consultancy website?

Students and parents look for evidence that real people at a real address have done this before. Vague success claims work against you, because families have heard them at every education fair.

The signals that carry weight:

- **Registration details.** Company registration, PAN and your Ministry of Education approval, stated plainly in the footer or on the About page.
- **Real counsellors.** Names, photos and the destinations each person handles. Mention certifications, such as PIER's Qualified Education Agent Counsellor programme for Australia or the British Council's agent training for the UK, only where a named counsellor holds them.
- **Partner institutions, used carefully.** Many agent agreements set rules on how you may use an institution's name and logo. Check yours before building a homepage wall of university crests.
- **Honest numbers, or none.** "100% visa success" cannot be verified and invites the obvious question. If you publish figures, say what they count and over what period.
- **Visible fees.** State what is free, what you charge for and what the institution charges directly.
- **Reviews in public places.** Google reviews on each branch's Business Profile carry more weight than quotes on your own homepage.

## How should a consultancy handle student documents safely?

Treat passports, transcripts, bank statements and sponsor documents as sensitive personal data. Collect them through an access-controlled upload, keep them in one place, and delete what you no longer need.

Nepal's Individual Privacy Act, 2075 (2018) expects personal information to be collected with consent and used for the purpose it was collected for. In practice that means:

- No passport or citizenship upload on the first enquiry form. Ask for documents after counselling, when there is a reason.
- A private upload link per student, stored in the CRM or a shared drive owned by the business, never in a counsellor's personal Gmail or chat history.
- Staff access limited by branch, with two-factor authentication on every account that can open student files.
- A written retention period, after which files for students who did not proceed are deleted.

This is also a sales point. Parents handing over bank statements notice when a consultancy asks for them over Viber.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do we need a custom CRM or will an off-the-shelf one do?

For one or two branches, an off-the-shelf CRM such as HubSpot or Zoho CRM with custom pipeline stages is usually enough and cheaper to run. A custom student enquiry system becomes worth it when you need permissions by branch, destination-specific document checklists, commission tracking per institution, or reporting that general CRMs make awkward. Start with the pipeline stages on paper; the right tool is easier to choose once those are agreed.

### Should our website show visa success rates?

Only if you can say exactly what the number counts: which destinations, which period, and whether withdrawn applications are included. Unqualified claims such as "100% visa success" are impossible to verify and make careful students and parents more suspicious, not less. Clear process pages, real counsellor profiles and Google reviews on each branch profile do more to build trust than a headline percentage.

### How often should destination pages be updated?

Check every destination page at least once per intake cycle, and immediately when a government changes a visa rule, financial requirement or English test policy. Put a "last checked" date and a link to the official source on each page. Students compare consultancies, and a page quoting an outdated requirement is often the reason they book a session somewhere else.

### Is it safe to let students upload documents through the website?

Yes, if uploads go to access-controlled storage owned by the business, links are private to each student, staff sign in with two-factor authentication and files are deleted after a set retention period. It is far safer than collecting passports and bank statements over email, Viber or WhatsApp, where copies end up on personal phones with no record of who has seen them.
