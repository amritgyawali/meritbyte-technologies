NGO website development is less about looking impressive and more about being checkable. A donor, grant officer or journalist should reach your registration details, leadership, annual reports and audited accounts within two clicks, and a supporter should be able to give in under a minute. For organisations working in Nepal, the same site often has to work in Nepali, on a slow rural connection.

## Who is an NGO website actually for?

Three audiences with different needs, and many sites serve only the first.

| Audience | What they look for | What the site needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Individual donors | Impact, where money goes, easy giving | Donation page, recurring gifts, clear use of funds |
| Institutional donors and partners | Registration, governance, audited reports, safeguarding policy | An accountability section with dated documents |
| Communities and beneficiaries | Services, eligibility, contacts in their language | Lightweight local-language pages, click-to-call |

Nonprofit website design goes wrong when the homepage is a slider of photos and the annual report is a 40 MB PDF buried three menus deep.

## What does a donation website need in each country?

Secure payment through a hosted provider, a receipt that satisfies local tax rules, and a record your finance team can reconcile. The receipt rules are the part generic templates miss.

| Market | What matters on the donation form |
| --- | --- |
| UK | Gift Aid declaration, so the charity can claim 25p per £1 from HMRC on eligible gifts; the Fundraising Regulator's code |
| USA | Written acknowledgement for single gifts of $250 or more to 501(c)(3) organisations |
| Australia | Receipts from DGR-endorsed charities; gifts of $2 or more may be tax-deductible |
| Canada | Official donation receipts carrying the details the CRA requires |
| Nepal | Registration with the District Administration Office and Social Welfare Council affiliation; foreign contributions follow approval and bank rules |

Stripe, PayPal, Donorbox and Givebutter all handle recurring giving well. For an organisation registered in Nepal, accepting international card donations directly depends on your bank's gateway and the approvals attached to your funding, so settle that with your bank and the Social Welfare Council before adding a donate button aimed at foreign supporters.

## How should donor data be handled?

Collect the minimum, keep it in one place and record consent. Donor lists are personal data under UK GDPR, Canada's PIPEDA (with CASL covering email), Australia's Privacy Act 1988 and Nepal's Individual Privacy Act 2075. Card details should never touch your server; the payment provider holds them.

In practice, that means a CRM such as CiviCRM, Salesforce Nonprofit Cloud or HubSpot instead of spreadsheets passed around by email, newsletter consent kept separate from the donation itself, and a personal login for every staff member. Our guide to [email consent and deliverability](/blog/email-marketing-for-small-business) covers the newsletter side.

## Building for low bandwidth and two languages

Field staff and beneficiaries in rural Nepal often use mid-range Android phones on unreliable mobile data. Design for them: a page-weight budget (under 1 MB for key pages is a sensible target), subsetted or system fonts, compressed images, no autoplay video, and an HTML summary of every report with the PDF as an optional download.

Bilingual content needs a plan, not a plugin. Use Unicode Devanagari such as Mukta or Noto Sans Devanagari (never Preeti), a separate URL per language, and a translation workflow that stops the Nepali pages drifting months behind the English ones. Our [Nepali-English bilingual website guide](/blog/nepali-english-bilingual-website) covers fonts and SEO. Build to WCAG 2.2 AA throughout, since donors, staff and beneficiaries include people using screen readers and magnification.

## NGO website development on a grant-funded budget

Grant money often arrives once, with a reporting deadline and no maintenance line. Spend it on things that outlast the project:

1. A content management system your communications officer can run without a developer, usually WordPress with a small, documented set of blocks.
2. Structured sections for programmes, reports and publications, so new projects slot in without a redesign.
3. The donation and receipt flow, tested end to end.
4. A year of hosting, backups and updates paid in advance and written into the proposal; our [website maintenance guide](/blog/website-maintenance-guide) explains what that covers.

Avoid spending grant funds on features only one project needs, or on a site only the original developer understands.

## What to build first

For a local NGO or an INGO website in Nepal, the first release should cover who you are and your registration, programmes and where you work, reports and accounts, safeguarding and contacts, and a donation page if you can lawfully accept gifts online. News, stories and a volunteer portal follow.

Once the site is live with HTTPS, a clear mission and substantial content, eligible organisations in countries where Google for Nonprofits operates can apply for Google Ad Grants, which provides up to US$10,000 a month in search advertising.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an NGO in Nepal accept online donations?

Domestic donations through eSewa, Khalti or a bank gateway are generally workable. Foreign donations are more involved: organisations receiving money from abroad have approval and reporting obligations, commonly through the Social Welfare Council, and banks apply their own checks. Confirm the rules for your registration with your bank and an adviser before launching an international donation page.

### Should an NGO website be built on WordPress?

Usually, yes. WordPress is widely known, cheap to host and quick for communications staff to learn, which matters when people change jobs. Keep plugins few and documented. A custom build only makes sense for something unusual, such as a beneficiary portal or a data-collection tool linked to your programme database.

### How do we make annual reports readable online?

Publish a short HTML version of each report on its own page: key figures, a few stories, where the money went, and links to the full PDF and audited accounts. HTML loads faster on mobile, works with screen readers, can be translated, and is far easier for search engines and AI assistants to read and quote.

### What does a nonprofit website cost to maintain?

Budget for hosting, domain renewal, security updates, backups and a few hours of support each month. The amount varies with the platform and how often the site changes. The bigger risk is budgeting nothing, because grant-funded sites can fall out of date and become insecure once the project that paid for them ends.
