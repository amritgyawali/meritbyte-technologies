## How do you keep a video-heavy site fast?

Stream the video instead of making the browser download a large file, and make sure something light appears first. Google's Core Web Vitals thresholds are LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms and CLS ≤ 0.1 at the 75th percentile of real visits, and a hero video is the most common reason a media-heavy site misses the first one.

1. Host with an adaptive streaming service such as Mux, Cloudflare Stream, Vimeo or YouTube, so each visitor gets a resolution their connection can handle.
2. Give every video a compressed poster image sized for its slot; the poster or first frame is often what LCP measures.
3. Keep background loops to a few muted seconds, and show a still image to visitors on slow connections or with reduced-motion settings.
4. Load below-the-fold videos only when they scroll into view, with `preload="none"` until then.
5. Reserve each video's space with a fixed aspect ratio so the layout does not jump.
6. Judge results from field data in Search Console, not a lab score on studio Wi-Fi.

## What does CCPA/CPRA mean for your pixels and forms?

The California Consumer Privacy Act, as amended by the CPRA, applies if your business meets any one threshold: annual gross revenue above an inflation-adjusted figure that started at $25 million; buying, selling or sharing the personal information of 100,000 or more California consumers or households; or earning half or more of annual revenue from selling or sharing it.

For a DTC brand, "sharing" is the trap. Sending browsing data to Meta, TikTok or Google for cross-context behavioral advertising counts, even when no money changes hands. In practice that means:

- A "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link that actually switches off advertising pixels.
- Honoring Global Privacy Control signals from the browser as an opt-out. The Attorney General's 2022 settlement with Sephora turned on exactly this point.
- A privacy policy describing what you collect and why, reviewed at least every 12 months.
- A "Limit the Use of My Sensitive Personal Information" link if you use sensitive data beyond what the regulations permit.

Two California exposures reach smaller businesses as well. Plaintiffs' firms have filed many suits under the California Invasion of Privacy Act over chat widgets, session-replay tools and tracking pixels, so every third-party script should be justified and disclosed. And the Unruh Civil Rights Act can let plaintiffs seek statutory damages of at least $4,000 per violation for accessibility barriers, which makes WCAG 2.2 AA work look cheap. Regulations adopted in 2025 also phase in risk assessments, cybersecurity audits and automated decision-making rules for larger businesses. Your counsel decides the policy; the developer makes the site obey it.

## How should an LA DTC brand use Shopify?

For most direct-to-consumer brands, Shopify is the right base, with custom work reserved for the theme, the product page and the few integrations that make the brand different. Fully custom e-commerce pays off only when the model genuinely does not fit, such as marketplace logic.

- **Drops and launches:** password or queue pages, inventory that cannot oversell, and bot protection for limited releases.
- **Subscriptions:** California's Automatic Renewal Law requires clear terms, affirmative consent and a simple way to cancel online, so configure the subscription app to match.
- **Sales tax:** rates differ between cities within LA County, so rely on Shopify's tax engine or a dedicated tax service rather than a flat rate.
- **Email and SMS:** Klaviyo or a similar platform, with consent captured and stored at checkout.

## Spanish-language pages that actually work

A large share of Los Angeles households speak Spanish at home, so a Spanish version is often a revenue decision rather than a courtesy. Do it properly or not at all:

1. Put Spanish pages on their own URLs, such as `/es/`, with hreflang tags for `en-US` and `es-US`.
2. Translate the whole journey: forms, confirmation emails, checkout, text messages and error states.
3. Use professional or native-speaker translation for product and service pages. Browser translation widgets are not indexed and often garble sizes, ingredients and prices.
4. Route Spanish inquiries to someone who can reply in Spanish.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does my Los Angeles business have to comply with the CCPA?

Only if you meet one of its thresholds: gross annual revenue above the inflation-adjusted limit that began at $25 million, buying, selling or sharing personal information of 100,000 or more California consumers or households, or earning half your revenue from selling or sharing it. Smaller businesses still face CIPA and accessibility claims, so tidy tracking pays off regardless.

### Should our home page use an autoplay hero video?

It can, if the clip is short, muted and streamed or heavily compressed, and a light poster image appears first. A large MP4 as the first thing on the page will usually fail the LCP threshold on mobile. A still image with a click-to-play reel is far easier to keep fast, and worth testing against the video version.

### Do we need a separate Spanish website?

Rarely. Spanish pages in their own subfolder, linked with hreflang, keep your search authority in one place and are simpler to maintain than a second domain. What you do need is full coverage of the buying journey, including checkout, emails and support, so a Spanish-speaking customer never hits an English-only dead end halfway through.

If in-person sessions on set or in your showroom are central to how you work, a local studio will suit you better. If written updates and a staging URL work for your team, the distance matters less than it sounds.
