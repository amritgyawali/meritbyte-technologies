## Does a Toronto business website have to meet AODA?

If your organization has 50 or more employees in Ontario, yes. The Integrated Accessibility Standards Regulation under the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act (AODA) requires the public websites of large private and non-profit organizations to conform to WCAG 2.0 Level AA, with exceptions only for live captions and pre-recorded audio descriptions.

Smaller organizations sit outside that rule, but their customers who use screen readers still want to book and buy. We recommend building to [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) Level AA, which the W3C designed to be backwards compatible, so a site that meets it also meets the version Ontario names.

In practice that means:

- Menus, date pickers and multi-step forms work with a keyboard alone, with a visible focus outline.
- Body text meets a 4.5:1 contrast ratio.
- Form errors are announced to screen readers, not only shown in red.
- Rate sheets and annual reports published as PDFs are tagged properly or rebuilt as HTML. Finance and professional-services sites often carry hundreds.
- Videos have captions.

Automated scanners such as axe catch only part of the problem; ask for a manual NVDA or VoiceOver test on a key page while you watch.

If your customers search in Chinese, Punjabi, Tamil, Tagalog or Portuguese, put each language on separate URLs, linked with hreflang, and have people, not machines, translate the pages that sell.

## PIPEDA, CASL and the forms on your site

Ontario has no general private-sector privacy statute of its own, so most Toronto business websites fall under the federal PIPEDA, while CASL governs the commercial emails and texts you send to the addresses those forms collect.

Under PIPEDA, a form should explain why each field is collected and ask for nothing you do not need. Breaches posing a real risk of significant harm have to be reported to the Privacy Commissioner of Canada and to the people affected. Bill C-27, which would have replaced PIPEDA, died when Parliament was prorogued in January 2025, so PIPEDA remains the standard in 2026.

CASL is stricter than most owners expect:

1. Express consent needs a positive action such as an unticked checkbox; a pre-ticked box does not count.
2. Keep a record of when, where and with what wording each person agreed.
3. Every message must identify you and carry an unsubscribe link that is honoured within 10 business days.
4. Implied consent expires: two years after a purchase, six months after an inquiry.

A developer should write that consent record into your CRM or email platform, not leave it buried in notification emails. If you also sell into Quebec, Law 25 goes further, for example by requiring profiling and tracking features to be off by default. ## How does the time difference with Nepal work for Toronto?

Nepal keeps UTC+5:45 year-round with no daylight saving. Against Toronto, that puts Nepal 9 hours 45 minutes ahead during Eastern Daylight Time and 10 hours 45 minutes ahead during Eastern Standard Time, switching when Ontario's clocks change in March and November.

## How do Toronto customers search for local businesses?

On their phones, by neighbourhood or by GTA city, often with "near me" attached. Ranking for "Toronto" alone matters less than appearing in the map results where your customers are.

Mississauga, Brampton, Vaughan and Markham are separate cities that people search by name. A service-area business should set its real service areas in Google Business Profile, earn reviews steadily, and publish a location page only where it has something specific to say; ten near-identical "plumber in Etobicoke" pages tend to do more harm than good. Neighbourhood names such as Leslieville and the Annex turn up in searches for restaurants, salons and clinics, so use them where they are true.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it risky to hire a web developer outside Canada for a Toronto business?

The risk sits in the contract and the process more than the location. Make sure the domain, hosting and code are in your name, that you can watch progress on a staging site, and that payments follow delivered milestones. Give the developer test data rather than real customer records wherever possible.

### Does a small Toronto business need to follow AODA?

AODA's website requirement applies to organizations with 50 or more employees in Ontario, which must meet WCAG 2.0 Level AA. Smaller businesses still have AODA customer service duties, and an inaccessible site turns customers away whatever your headcount. Building to WCAG 2.2 AA costs little extra when it is planned from the start rather than retrofitted later.

### Can you build an English and French website for a Toronto company?

Yes. Each language should live at its own URLs, with hreflang annotations telling Google which version to show to whom. Ontario does not require French on private business sites, but Quebec customers and federal clients may expect it. Budget for professional translation, because machine-translated French reads badly.
