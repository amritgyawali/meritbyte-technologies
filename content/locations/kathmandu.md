## Why is it hard to stand out online in Kathmandu?

Because your nearest competitors are often a few doors away. Thamel packs hotels, guesthouses and trekking agencies into a handful of lanes; Putalisadak has education consultancies stacked floor above floor; New Road has street after street of shops selling the same phones and laptops.

Google says it ranks local results on relevance, distance and prominence. In a city this dense, distance barely separates you from the office next door, so relevance (a complete, accurate profile and pages that say exactly what you do) and prominence (reviews, links, mentions) decide who appears in the top three on Maps. A generic five-page brochure site does little for either.

## How do people in Kathmandu search for a business?

Mostly on phones, often in a mix of languages, and usually ending in a chat rather than a form. The patterns below are typical; the wording varies, but the channel at the end rarely does.

| Kathmandu business | What people type | Where the enquiry usually lands |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trekking agency in Thamel | "everest base camp trek cost", "langtang trek 7 days" (mostly from abroad) | Email, WhatsApp |
| Hotel in Thamel or Lazimpat | "hotel near thamel", "boutique hotel kathmandu" | OTA listing or direct booking form |
| Education consultancy in Putalisadak | "study in australia consultancy", "ielts class putalisadak", Devanagari queries | Facebook Messenger, phone call |
| Restaurant or café | "momo near me", "rooftop restaurant thamel" | A call or directions from Google Maps |
| Electronics shop on New Road | "laptop price in nepal", exact model numbers | Viber, Facebook, a shop visit |

Two local details catch out developers from elsewhere. Many people search in Romanised Nepali ("sasto mobile", "kotha bhada"), so pages should use the words customers use, not only formal English. And Nepali content must be real Unicode Devanagari: text typed in a legacy font such as Preeti looks right on the office PC but is gibberish to Google and to most phones. ## What to look for in a website developer in Kathmandu

The best website developer in Kathmandu for your business is the one who asks about your enquiry channels before showing you templates. Use this checklist in the first conversation:

1. They set up or clean up your Google Business Profile: correct category, ward-level address, opening hours, photos and a plan for collecting reviews.
2. They build mobile-first and show Core Web Vitals measured on a phone (LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1), not a desktop screenshot.
3. Nepali text is Unicode, set in a web font such as Mukta or Noto Sans Devanagari, and the English and Nepali pages have separate URLs.
4. Click-to-call, Viber and WhatsApp links work from every page, and Messenger enquiries are counted rather than guessed.
5. Payments run through a proper eSewa, Khalti, Fonepay QR or ConnectIPS merchant account, tested end to end on staging.
6. The domain, hosting, Google accounts and code are registered in your name.
7. They plan pages for the searches in the table above instead of one catch-all "Services" page.
8. They tell you what they will not do, such as buying links or promising a first-page ranking.

If a developer cannot explain points 3 and 5 in plain words, keep looking. The national [15-point checklist for choosing a developer in Nepal](/blog/best-website-developer-in-nepal-checklist) goes further.

## What do Kathmandu's main sectors need from a website?

### Thamel hotels and trekking agencies

Your buyers are mostly abroad and comparing you with a dozen agencies selling the same itinerary. Trip pages need day-by-day plans, what is included, permit details, fixed departure dates, a deposit option and honest photos. Hotels need a booking engine so guests can book direct instead of through an OTA. Our page on [travel and tourism websites](/industries/travel-and-tourism) has the detail.

### Putalisadak education consultancies

Students and parents compare consultancies on Facebook first, then check the website for proof: which universities you represent, class schedules, fees for IELTS or PTE preparation, and a named counsellor. An enquiry form that asks for intake, course and test score, and sends leads into a simple CRM, beats a bare contact page. The post on [education consultancy websites in Nepal](/blog/education-consultancy-website-nepal) walks through the funnel.

### Restaurants, cafés and retail

For a restaurant in Durbarmarg or Boudha, the website mostly feeds Google: the menu as text rather than a photo of a laminated card, hours, a map pin and a phone number. For shops in New Road or Baneshwor, the real decision is between a catalogue that pushes enquiries to Viber and a proper online store with stock levels and payment. The store is more work; it pays off if you already sell through Facebook every day.

## Payments, domains and the legal basics in Kathmandu

- **Payments.** eSewa, Khalti and Fonepay QR are what customers expect; ConnectIPS suits larger bank transfers. The providers are licensed by Nepal Rastra Bank and each runs its own merchant onboarding. International cards need a Nepali bank's card gateway, because Stripe does not onboard Nepal-registered businesses. See our [eSewa, Khalti and Fonepay integration guide](/blog/esewa-khalti-fonepay-integration).
- **Domains.** A .com.np is free for eligible Nepali businesses and individuals; a .com is often the better main domain if most customers are abroad.
- **Privacy.** The Individual Privacy Act 2075 (2018) covers personal data you collect through forms, and the Electronic Transactions Act 2063 (2008) covers electronic records and online transactions. In practice: a plain-language privacy notice, forms that ask only for what you need, and admin logins protected with two-factor sign-in.
- **Prices.** Say whether prices include 13% VAT, and for restaurants whether the 10% service charge is added on top.

## Working with a Nepal-based team from Kathmandu

There is no time-zone planning to do: Nepal runs on UTC+5:45 all year with no daylight saving. What needs planning is the calendar. Dashain and Tihar fall in autumn, which is also peak trekking season in Thamel, and the fiscal year ends in mid-July, when many organisations commit remaining budgets. A trekking agency's new site should go live before September, not in the middle of October.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a Kathmandu-based developer to rank in Kathmandu?

No. Google ranks your business on your Business Profile, your site's content and your reputation, not on where your developer sits. What matters is that the developer understands how Kathmandu customers search, sets up the profile properly and writes pages for the queries people actually use. A developer nearby helps only if you want frequent in-person meetings.

### Should my Kathmandu business website be in Nepali or English?

Usually both, weighted by who your customers are. Thamel hotels and trekking agencies sell mostly to foreigners, so English leads. Consultancies, clinics, schools and shops serving local families benefit from Nepali pages as well, especially for parents. Put each language on its own URLs in Unicode so both can rank, rather than mixing languages on one page.

### How long does a small business website take in Kathmandu?

A straightforward business site of five to ten pages typically takes four to eight weeks, and the biggest variable is how quickly content and photos arrive. eSewa or Khalti payments, a booking engine or a bilingual build add time, because merchant onboarding and translation run on their own schedules. Avoid planning a launch for the Dashain and Tihar weeks.

### Is a Facebook page enough for a Kathmandu business?

Facebook is where many Kathmandu customers start, but it is rented ground: reach changes, pages get hacked, and Google rarely shows Facebook posts for searches like "study in australia consultancy". A website gives you pages that rank, a place to take payments and records you control. Most businesses need both, linked to each other.
