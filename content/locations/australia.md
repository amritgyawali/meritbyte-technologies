## Which Australian laws shape a business website in 2026?

Four pieces of legislation account for most of the compliance work on a typical Australian site.

**Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles.** Businesses with annual turnover above $3 million are covered, and so are some smaller ones, including health service providers and businesses that trade in personal information. If you are covered and an overseas developer can see customer data, APP 8 applies: you remain accountable for how that overseas recipient handles it. The sensible setup is test data on staging, production access limited to named people, and a data-handling clause in the contract. Covered entities also fall under the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme.

**Spam Act 2003.** Commercial emails and SMS need consent, accurate sender identification and a working unsubscribe. The Australian Communications and Media Authority (ACMA) enforces it, so pre-ticked boxes and purchased lists are risks, not shortcuts.

**Disability Discrimination Act 1992.** It applies to websites; the complaint over the Sydney 2000 Olympics website, *Maguire v SOCOG*, established that. The Australian Human Rights Commission's guidance points to WCAG.

**Australian Consumer Law.** Beyond GST-inclusive total prices, your checkout should not suggest customers have fewer rights than the consumer guarantees give them.

## What does a .com.au, GST and payments setup look like?

Most Australian business sites need the same three foundations, and each has to be in your name before launch.

- **Domain.** auDA's rules require an Australian presence, such as an ABN, ACN or Australian trade mark, for .com.au and the shorter .au names available since 2022. You register it; your developer configures DNS under your account.
- **GST.** GST is 10%. Consumer prices on the site include it; B2B price lists may show it separately if labelled clearly.
- **Payments.** Stripe and PayPal cover cards and wallets; Afterpay and Zip add buy now, pay later; Square suits businesses already using its terminals. For e-commerce, Australia Post and Sendle both offer shipping integrations for Shopify and WooCommerce.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a Nepal-based developer register a .com.au domain for me?

Not in their own name, and they should not try. Eligibility for .com.au depends on your Australian presence, usually your ABN or ACN, so you register the domain through an accredited registrar under your business account. Your developer can then be given access to manage DNS, while ownership stays with you if you ever change developers.

### Does the Privacy Act apply to my small business website?

Possibly not, but check before assuming. Businesses with annual turnover of $3 million or less are generally exempt, except health service providers, businesses that trade in personal information and a few other categories. Even when exempt, a clear privacy notice and minimal data collection are good practice, and the Spam Act applies to your email marketing regardless of size.

### What hours can we actually talk?

A Nepal morning meets your afternoon. In winter, 2 p.m. in Sydney or Melbourne is 9:45 a.m. in Nepal; during daylight saving it is 8:45 a.m. Brisbane stays at 4h15m ahead all year, and Perth, 2h15m ahead, overlaps more than half of a Nepal working day. Demos are booked in that window.

### Should my online store offer Afterpay?

Offer it if your average order value and customer base suit buy now, pay later: fashion, homewares, beauty and gifts commonly do, while low-margin or B2B stores often do not. Merchant fees for BNPL are higher than for ordinary card payments, so compare your margins before switching it on, and measure conversion for a few months afterwards.
