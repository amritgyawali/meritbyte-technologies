A Patan metalwork exporter needs a wholesale buyer in Europe or the US to trust a supplier they have never met. A café in Jhamsikhel needs to appear when someone nearby searches for brunch. ## Which kind of Lalitpur website are you building?

Most Lalitpur projects fall into one of three types. Trying to serve all three goals on one site is a common reason sites underperform.

| Type | Main visitor | What the site must do | What to get right |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Handicraft or metalwork exporter | Wholesale buyer or retailer abroad | Show range, process and trade terms; generate enquiries or orders | Product data, international payments, shipping, buyer-grade English |
| Café, restaurant or shop | Locals, expats and visitors nearby | Get found on Maps, show the menu, take calls or bookings | Google Business Profile, fast mobile pages, accurate hours |
| NGO or INGO | Donors, partners, government, job seekers | Show credibility, results and accountability | Accessibility, reports, bilingual content, beneficiary privacy |

## Selling Patan metalwork and handicrafts to buyers abroad

Patan's craft trade, from lost-wax cast statues to jewellery, woodcarving and textiles, sells mostly to people who will never visit the workshop. The website has to do the job a trade-fair stand would. A wholesale buyer looks for, roughly in this order:

- product pages with dimensions, weight, metal or material, finish options and SKU codes, not just a photo gallery;
- minimum order quantities, typical lead times and whether you take custom designs;
- process pages showing casting, chasing and gilding, which also attract collectors searching for how pieces are made;
- shipping terms: whether you quote EXW, FOB or delivered, which couriers you use and how pieces are packed;
- a trade price list or quote form, behind a short buyer registration if pricing is wholesale only.

Payment is the awkward part. Stripe and Shopify Payments are not available to businesses registered in Nepal, so exporters usually combine a Nepali bank's card-acceptance gateway for retail orders with proforma invoices paid by bank transfer for wholesale. A developer should explain those routes, and the foreign-exchange documents your bank will ask for, rather than promise a one-click global checkout that cannot be switched on. Our guide to [starting an e-commerce website in Nepal](/blog/start-ecommerce-website-in-nepal) covers the platform decision.

If you sell in more than one language or target several countries, settle the [international SEO](/blog/international-seo-guide) structure (one domain or several, hreflang) before the build, not after.

## Cafés and restaurants in Jhamsikhel, Sanepa and Kupondole

For a café, the website is a supporting act; Google Maps and Instagram do most of the persuading. The site's job is to be the reliable source both of them, and your customers, check:

- a menu Google can read, with prices that say whether 13% VAT and the 10% service charge are included;
- opening hours that match the Business Profile exactly, including festival closures;
- a map pin that lands on the right lane in Jhamsikhel, not on the Ring Road;
- a group and event booking form, because a phone that rings out during the lunch rush loses the table of twelve.

With a sizeable expat and NGO clientele in these neighbourhoods, English pages usually lead, with Nepali where your regulars expect it. Keep the pages light: a menu uploaded as a heavy PDF is a common reason people on mobile data give up.

## NGO and INGO websites around Pulchowk, Sanepa and Jawalakhel

Lalitpur hosts a large part of Nepal's development sector, from UN House in Pulchowk to INGO country offices in Sanepa, Jawalakhel and Kupondole. Their sites are read by people who are checking rather than browsing: donors running due diligence, government counterparts, partner organisations and job applicants.

A donor-ready site usually includes:

1. Registration details: District Administration Office registration and Social Welfare Council affiliation for NGOs, or the SWC general agreement for INGOs.
2. Annual reports and audited financial statements, with an accessible HTML summary next to each PDF.
3. Governance pages: board, safeguarding, anti-fraud and complaints policies, and a route for reporting concerns.
4. Programme pages with locations, partners and results, which your own staff can update through a simple CMS.
5. Accessibility to WCAG 2.2 level AA, which many donors expect and which is non-negotiable for disability-focused programmes.
6. Low-bandwidth pages for field staff and partners on weak district connections.

Beneficiary data needs care. Photos and stories, especially of children, need recorded consent, and the Individual Privacy Act 2075 (2018) applies to personal data collected through your forms. If you plan to accept donations online, confirm which payment routes your registration permits before anything is built. Our page on [NGO and nonprofit websites](/industries/nonprofits) goes deeper.

## Patan or Lalitpur? Local search in a city with two names

People search using both. Visitors and many Nepali users type "Patan", official addresses say Lalitpur, and some searches use the neighbourhood alone ("jhamel cafe", "pulchowk office space"). A developer who knows the city will:

- use Lalitpur in the structured address and Business Profile, and Patan in headings and copy where people would naturally say it;
- mention the landmarks customers navigate by, such as Patan Durbar Square, Mangal Bazaar, Jawalakhel Chowk or Pulchowk Campus;
- write separate pages only for genuinely different services, not one page per neighbourhood with the name swapped.

Homestays and guesthouses in the old city gain the most. Patan Durbar Square is part of the Kathmandu Valley UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the visitors it draws search in English and book on their phones. ## What to look for in a website developer in Lalitpur

Beyond a portfolio, ask questions that match your type of organisation:

- **Exporters:** "How will a buyer in Germany pay us, and what will our bank need?" "How do we manage specifications for several hundred products?"
- **Cafés and shops:** "Will you set up and match our Google Business Profile?" "How fast is the menu page on a phone?"
- **NGOs:** "How do you test for WCAG 2.2 AA?" "Can programme staff update pages without calling you?" "Where is donor and beneficiary data stored?"
- **Everyone:** "Is Nepali text Unicode?" "Are the domain, hosting and code in our organisation's name?" "What does support cost after launch?"

A developer who answers with specifics, and admits what depends on your bank or your donor, is worth more than one with a glossier portfolio. For speed targets, our explainer on [Core Web Vitals](/blog/core-web-vitals-explained) sets out the thresholds.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a Lalitpur handicraft exporter take card payments from abroad?

Yes, though usually not through Stripe or Shopify Payments, neither of which accepts Nepal-registered businesses. The common routes are a card-acceptance gateway from a Nepali bank for retail orders and proforma invoices paid by bank transfer for wholesale. Your bank sets the documentation requirements, so bring them in before the developer builds the checkout.

### What makes an NGO website "donor-ready"?

It answers a due-diligence reviewer's questions without an email: registration and Social Welfare Council details, audited accounts, governance and safeguarding policies, programme results and a way to report concerns. It also meets WCAG 2.2 AA, loads on slow connections, and can be updated by your own staff so it does not go stale between grants.

### Does a Patan café need a website if it has Instagram?

A small one, yes. Instagram shows the atmosphere, but Google Maps and search results rely on your website and Business Profile for menus, hours and prices. A single fast page with the menu as text, correct hours, the map pin and a phone number often does more for walk-ins than extra posts, and costs little to maintain.

### Should we use "Patan" or "Lalitpur" on our website?

Both. Use Lalitpur in your formal address, Business Profile and structured data, because it is the official name. Use Patan in headings and copy where customers would say it, especially for tourism and heritage businesses. Searchers use both names, and your pages are easier to match when they mention the two naturally.
