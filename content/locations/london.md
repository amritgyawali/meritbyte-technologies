## What do London businesses need from a web developer?

Each sector brings its own rules.

- **Fintech and financial services:** product pages, onboarding journeys, app landing pages, calculators and help centres, often with Open Banking or card payments behind them.
- **Law firms:** the SRA Transparency Rules require firms offering certain services, such as residential conveyancing, probate and immigration work, to publish price and service information, and SRA-regulated firms must display the SRA digital badge.
- **Accountants and consultancies:** service pages, partner profiles, online booking, and a client portal so documents stop travelling as email attachments.
- **Hospitality and retail:** menus, table bookings and online shops showing VAT-inclusive prices.

Every UK company website also needs the details required by the Companies (Trading Disclosures) Regulations 2008: registered name, company number, part of the UK where registered, and registered office address, usually in the footer.

Search behaviour is local too. Londoners look for services by area or postcode district ("accountant Clapham", "physio EC2"), so your Google Business Profile, categories and reviews matter more than a page claiming to serve all of London.

## UK GDPR, PECR and the ICO: what does the build have to get right?

Non-essential cookies and similar tracking must not run until the visitor agrees, rejecting must be as easy as accepting, and what your forms collect must match what your privacy notice says. The Information Commissioner's Office regulates both UK GDPR and PECR, and it has been contacting website operators about cookie banners that fall short.

A checklist for the developer:

1. Analytics, advertising tags and embedded players load only after consent. Test this in a clean browser with the network panel open, not by reading the banner's settings.
2. "Reject all" sits on the first layer of the banner with the same prominence as "Accept all".
3. Google Consent Mode v2 is set up if you run Google Ads or GA4 for UK visitors.
4. Each form states its purpose, links to the privacy notice and keeps marketing consent separate from the enquiry.
5. Marketing emails follow PECR: prior consent, except where the soft opt-in covers existing customers, and an unsubscribe link in every message.
6. Personal data is kept only as long as your retention schedule allows.

The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 amends parts of UK GDPR and PECR, with changes commenced in stages, so read the ICO's latest guidance before assuming any cookie no longer needs consent.

Using a developer outside the UK adds a transfer question, covered in the FAQ below. The simplest answer is often to keep developers on staging with synthetic data, with production access limited and logged.

## How can a website support FCA sign-off on financial promotions?

By treating every page, email and social post that invites someone into financial activity as a financial promotion that needs recorded approval, and building the content management system around that. Under section 21 of the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000, a financial promotion must be made or approved by an authorised person, and FCA rules require it to be fair, clear and not misleading.

What that means for the build:

- **Separate roles.** Editors draft; only named approvers in compliance can publish pages tagged as promotions.
- **Locked approved content.** Any edit to an approved page sends it back for approval, and the change log records who changed what, and when.
- **Archived versions.** Every published version is stored, so you can show exactly what a customer saw on a given date.
- **Risk warnings as components.** Required warnings are built as components that editors cannot delete or restyle. For high-risk investments and cryptoasset promotions, FCA rules set prominence requirements for risk warnings, including keeping them visible as the user scrolls, and a 24-hour cooling-off period for first-time investors has to be built into the journey.
- **Preview links,** so compliance reviews the real page on staging rather than a screenshot.

A headless CMS with role-based workflows often fits this better than a standard WordPress install; our [headless CMS guide](/blog/headless-cms-guide) explains the trade-off. The Consumer Duty also expects communications that customers can understand, which favours plain, well-structured pages. We build the workflow; your compliance team and approver make the calls.

## How much of the London working day overlaps with Nepal?

A good part of your morning. Under British Summer Time the gap is 4 hours 45 minutes; under Greenwich Mean Time it is 5 hours 45 minutes, because Nepal never changes its clocks and the UK does, on the last Sunday of March and of October.

| London | Nepal, during BST | Nepal, during GMT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 09:00 | 13:45 | 14:45 |
| 11:00 | 15:45 | 16:45 |
| 13:00 | 17:45 | 18:45 |
| 17:30 | 22:15 | 23:15 |

So calls between 09:00 and about 13:00 in summer, or 09:00 and 12:00 in winter, fall in the Nepal afternoon, a question raised before lunch can often be answered the same day, and a request sent later is picked up the next Nepal morning, still before London's commute. The trade-off is milder than with North America but real: no face-to-face workshops in the City or Shoreditch.

## What to look for in a web developer in London

1. Open your current site in a private window: does any tracking fire before you accept? A good developer checks this before quoting.
2. Ask how they would handle an approval workflow for regulated content, and whether edits after approval are logged.
3. If they are outside the UK, ask which transfer mechanism they will use and whether they will sign a processor contract.
4. Ask which accessibility standard they build to (WCAG 2.2 AA is the sensible target) and how they test it; the Equality Act 2010 expects reasonable adjustments for disabled users.
5. Confirm the domain, including your .co.uk, plus hosting, analytics and code are registered to your company.
6. Ask how you will see work in progress, and how often it is updated.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it lawful for a Nepal-based developer to access our customers' personal data?

Yes, if the transfer is covered. Because Nepal has no UK adequacy regulations, you need a processor contract plus either the ICO's International Data Transfer Agreement or its UK Addendum to the EU standard contractual clauses, and a transfer risk assessment. Many projects avoid most of this by giving developers anonymised or synthetic data and restricting production access.

### Do we need a cookie banner if we only use Google Analytics?

In most cases, yes. PECR has generally required consent for analytics cookies, and the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 creates only narrow exemptions for some analytics, with conditions and staged commencement. Until the ICO's guidance confirms your setup qualifies, ask for consent, block the tags until it is given, and use Consent Mode so reporting degrades gracefully.

### Can you build a website for an FCA-regulated firm?

Yes, in the sense of building the site and its publishing controls: role-based approval, locked approved content, version archives and risk warnings editors cannot remove. We are not your compliance function. Deciding what counts as a financial promotion, drafting risk warnings and approving content remain with your firm and its approver, and we build to what they decide.

### Can you take over a site another London agency built?

Usually. The first step is an audit: who holds the domain, hosting and code, which plugins or packages are out of date, and what is undocumented. Accounts are moved into your company's name before any rebuild. If a rebuild is needed, redirects are mapped page by page so existing search rankings are protected.
