## Why do so many ADA website lawsuits land in New York?

The Southern and Eastern Districts of New York are among the busiest federal courts for website cases under the Americans with Disabilities Act (Title III), and plaintiffs often add claims under the New York State and New York City human rights laws, which, unlike Title III, can allow damages. Retail and restaurant sites are frequent targets.

Private business websites have no federally codified technical standard. The Justice Department's 2024 rule adopting WCAG 2.1 Level AA covers state and local governments, not private companies, but demand letters and settlements routinely use WCAG 2.1 AA as the yardstick. Building to [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) AA covers it.

What a developer should do, rather than promise:

- Test key journeys (navigation, search, checkout, booking, contact) by keyboard alone and with JAWS, NVDA and VoiceOver.
- Replace menu and price-list PDFs with HTML. An image-only PDF menu is exactly the kind of barrier a complaint points to.
- Run automated axe checks in the deployment pipeline so fixed issues stay fixed.
- Publish an accessibility statement with a working way to report problems.

Overlay widgets that promise one-line compliance leave the underlying code unchanged, and sites running them still get sued.

## What does the SHIELD Act require of your website?

If you own or license private information about New York residents, wherever your business is based, you must keep reasonable administrative, technical and physical safeguards and notify affected people after a breach. For websites, "private information" includes a username or email address combined with a password or security answer, as well as account or card numbers with their access codes and biometric data.

The Act scales expectations to size: a small business (fewer than 50 employees, under $3 million in gross annual revenue in each of the last three fiscal years, or under $5 million in year-end total assets) needs safeguards appropriate to its size and complexity. For a typical site, reasonable looks like this:

1. Passwords hashed with Argon2 or bcrypt, never stored or emailed in plain text.
2. Multi-factor authentication on every admin, hosting and registrar account.
3. Plugins and dependencies patched on a schedule, with a named owner.
4. Old form submissions deleted on a schedule instead of piling up in the CMS.
5. Access removed the day a contractor or employee leaves.

Firms licensed by the New York Department of Financial Services also fall under its cybersecurity regulation, 23 NYCRR Part 500, which requires policies for third-party service providers. Your developer is one, so expect to send a security questionnaire. And if your product uses AI to screen job candidates for roles in New York City, Local Law 144 adds bias-audit and notice requirements. ## What to look for in a web developer in New York

Ask these before you sign, and write down the answers:

1. Which WCAG version do you build to, and which screen readers do you test with?
2. If we receive an ADA demand letter, what can you hand our lawyer within a week? An audit and a dated remediation log is the right answer.
3. How are passwords stored, and who will hold admin access to our site and hosting?
4. Will you complete our vendor security questionnaire, and who signs it?
5. Whose name is on the domain registrar, hosting account and code repository?
6. Where can we see work in progress, and how often is it updated?

## Frequently asked questions

### Will an accessibility overlay protect my New York business from ADA lawsuits?

No. Overlays add a toolbar on top of the page but leave the underlying code unchanged, and sites using them continue to receive demand letters and lawsuits. The durable fix is to correct the markup, contrast, focus order, forms and documents so the site meets WCAG 2.1 or 2.2 Level AA, then keep testing with screen readers as the site changes.

### Does the SHIELD Act apply if my business is not in New York?

Yes, if you own or license private information about New York residents. The law follows the data rather than your address, so an out-of-state online store holding customer accounts for New Yorkers is covered. Its security requirement is scaled for small businesses, but you still need reasonable safeguards and a plan for notifying people after a breach.

### Can you fix our existing site after a demand letter?

Usually, yes, once your lawyer is involved. The work starts with an audit of the pages and journeys named in the letter, then the rest of the site, and issues are fixed in priority order with a dated log your lawyer can use. Remediation is often faster than a rebuild, though very old themes or page builders sometimes make a rebuild the cheaper route.

### Can you release changes before New York's working day starts?

Yes, and the time difference helps. A release scheduled for 7:00 a.m. Eastern falls at 4:45 p.m. in Nepal during daylight time and 5:45 p.m. in winter, so changes can go live and be checked before most of your customers are awake. Anything risky is tested on staging first, where you approve it the day before.
