## Which privacy law applies: PIPEDA, Law 25 or a provincial act?

PIPEDA governs private-sector organizations' commercial activity in most provinces. Quebec, British Columbia and Alberta have their own private-sector privacy laws that apply within those provinces, while PIPEDA still covers data crossing provincial or national borders.

Quebec's Law 25 has the most direct effect on a website:

- The person in charge of protecting personal information (by default the CEO) must be named, with contact details published on the site.
- Visitors must be told about technology that can identify, locate or profile them and given the means to switch it on, which in practice means opt-in consent for advertising and most analytics cookies on Quebec-facing sites.
- A privacy impact assessment is required before personal information is communicated outside Quebec, and that includes giving an overseas developer access to it.

That last point is why we recommend test data on staging and tightly limited production access for any Quebec client. Under PIPEDA, you stay accountable for data a vendor processes, and breaches posing a real risk of significant harm must be reported to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner.

## Why is CASL stricter than other anti-spam laws?

Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation, in force since 2014, is opt-in: you need express or implied consent before sending a commercial electronic message, and administrative penalties reach $10 million per violation for businesses.

What that means on a website:

- Consent checkboxes are never pre-ticked, and the wording shown is stored with the consent record.
- Implied consent from a purchase lasts two years; from an inquiry, six months. Your CRM should track the expiry.
- Every message identifies the sender, gives contact information and carries an unsubscribe link that keeps working for 60 days after sending.

## Does your website need a French version?

If you carry on business in Quebec, generally yes. The Charter of the French Language, strengthened by Bill 96 in 2022, requires commercial information to be available in French, and any other-language version should not be more complete or prominent. Outside Quebec, French is a commercial choice rather than a legal one for most private businesses.

A bilingual build done properly uses separate URLs such as `/fr/` and `/en/`, hreflang tags for fr-CA and en-CA, translated slugs and metadata, and French versions of the cookie banner, emails, form errors and checkout. French copy often runs longer than English, so navigation and buttons need room. ## What does AODA require from an Ontario website?

Under the Integrated Accessibility Standards Regulation, private and non-profit organizations in Ontario with 50 or more employees must make public websites conform to WCAG 2.0 Level AA, excluding live captions and pre-recorded audio descriptions. The requirement has applied since January 1, 2021.

Smaller Ontario organizations are not bound by that website rule, though other AODA standards, such as customer service, apply to organizations with even one employee. Federally regulated sectors like banking and telecommunications fall under the Accessible Canada Act, and provincial human rights codes apply everywhere. Building to WCAG 2.2 AA, from colour contrast to keyboard navigation, covers all of these at once.

## Payments, tax and pricing in Canadian dollars

Stripe, Shopify Payments, PayPal, Moneris and Helcim all process cards in CAD, and Interac e-Transfer is common for paying B2B invoices. GST is 5% federally; Ontario and the Atlantic provinces charge HST, while British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba add PST and Quebec adds QST. Canadian shoppers expect prices before tax with the total at checkout, the opposite of Australia or the UK.

If you also sell to the US, show CAD by default and let American visitors switch, rather than making Canadians guess the exchange rate. ## Frequently asked questions

### Does CASL apply to B2B emails?

Mostly, yes. CASL covers commercial electronic messages whether the recipient is a consumer or a business. Narrow exemptions exist, and implied consent can arise when someone conspicuously publishes a business email address without saying they do not want messages, provided your message relates to their role. Bought B2B lists rarely meet those conditions, so build consented lists.

### Can a Nepal-based team work with a Vancouver company?

Yes, but mostly asynchronously. Nepal is 12h45m ahead of Pacific daylight time, so a 5 p.m. Vancouver call is 5:45 a.m. in Nepal. We rely on written specs, the weekly written update and the staging site, with a live demo booked in a narrow early or late window every two weeks.

### Does AODA apply to my small Ontario business?

The website requirement applies to private and non-profit organizations with 50 or more employees in Ontario. Below that, AODA's customer service standard still applies, and human rights obligations do not depend on size. Building to WCAG 2.2 AA from the start costs little extra and means you will not need a rebuild as you grow.

### Where will my customers' data be stored?

Wherever you decide. AWS, Azure and Google Cloud all run Canadian regions, including Montréal and Toronto, and Shopify and most SaaS tools publish where they hold data. Quebec clients sharing personal information outside the province must complete a privacy impact assessment first, so we plan hosting and developer access with that in mind.
