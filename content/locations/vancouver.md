## How does a 12h45m time difference work for a Vancouver project?

Mostly asynchronously. Nepal (UTC+5:45, no daylight saving) is 12 hours 45 minutes ahead of Vancouver on Pacific Daylight Time and 13 hours 45 minutes ahead on Pacific Standard Time, so each day offers two short windows for live calls rather than a shared afternoon.

| Window | Vancouver, PDT (March to November) | Nepal | Vancouver, PST (November to March) | Nepal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Early | 7:00 to 8:30 a.m. | 7:45 to 9:15 p.m. | 7:00 to 8:00 a.m. | 8:45 to 9:45 p.m. |
| Late | 7:00 to 8:30 p.m. | 7:45 to 9:15 a.m. next day | 6:00 to 7:30 p.m. | 7:45 to 9:15 a.m. next day |

One scheduled call a week is usually plenty. The rest depends on habits that make distance cheap:

- A written spec for each two-week block, agreed before work starts.
- Short screen recordings instead of meetings for design reviews.
- Comments pinned to the page on staging or in Figma, not scattered across email.
- A list of blocking questions sent at the end of each Nepal working day, so your answers are waiting by the next morning.

The honest trade-off: if you like to phone your developer at 2 p.m. and see the fix by 3 p.m., this arrangement will frustrate you. What you get instead is a full day of progress to review before your first meeting, at a cost shaped by Nepal's economy rather than Vancouver's.

## Which privacy law applies to a Vancouver website: PIPA or PIPEDA?

For most BC businesses, it is the province's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA), overseen by the Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner for British Columbia. PIPEDA still applies when personal information crosses provincial or national borders in the course of commercial activity, and to federally regulated businesses such as banks and airlines.

What PIPA changes for a build:

- **Employee and applicant data is covered.** A careers portal for a studio or tech company falls under PIPA, so application forms need the same consent and retention thinking as customer forms.
- **Purposes must be explained.** Say at the point of collection what each field is for, and keep marketing consent separate from the inquiry itself.
- **People can ask what you hold.** Store submissions somewhere searchable and exportable so you can answer an access request without digging through inboxes.
- **Providers outside Canada are allowed, with care.** Your privacy policy should say that service providers in other countries may process data, and your contract should require safeguards.

If you sell to BC public bodies such as health authorities or universities, they answer to a different law, FIPPA, and their procurement teams will ask pointed questions about where data sits and who can reach it from outside Canada. Plan hosting in a Canadian region from day one in that case. Anti-spam rules are federal, so CASL governs your newsletters exactly as it does in Ontario; our [Canada page](/website-developer/canada) explains CASL alongside Quebec's Law 25.

## Selling across the Pacific from a Vancouver site

Many Vancouver businesses sell to customers in Asia, or to Asian-Canadian communities at home. A site built only in English, priced only in CAD, leaves much of that trade on the table.

1. **Pick the right Chinese.** Traditional characters (zh-Hant) suit many Cantonese-speaking and Taiwanese readers; Simplified (zh-Hans) suits mainland readers. Getting it wrong looks careless.
2. **Give each language its own URLs**, such as `/zh-hant/` and `/pa/`, with hreflang tags. Punjabi, widely spoken in Surrey, is written in Gurmukhi and needs a font that renders it cleanly.
3. **Show local currencies and payment methods.** Shopify Markets and Stripe both support multi-currency pricing, and Stripe offers Asian wallets such as Alipay in some markets; confirm availability for your account.
4. **Handle tax and duties in the open.** BC shoppers expect 5% GST and 7% PST calculated correctly at checkout, and overseas buyers want to know whether duties are included before they pay.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will I ever talk to the developers live?

Yes, on a schedule. The practical windows are early morning or early evening in Vancouver, which fall in the Nepal evening or the next Nepal morning. Most clients book one call a week in one of those windows and handle everything else through written updates, short recorded walkthroughs and comments left directly on the staging site.

### Can the site be in Chinese and Punjabi as well as English?

Yes. Each language gets its own URLs and hreflang tags, and the design is tested with the real scripts, since Chinese characters and Gurmukhi need suitable fonts and line heights. Use native-speaker translation for the pages that sell. Machine translation is fine for drafts, but readers notice it quickly on product and service pages.

### Does BC PIPA stop me using a developer outside Canada?

No. PIPA does not ban service providers abroad, but you stay responsible for the personal information they handle. Put security terms in the contract, give the developer only the access they need, prefer test data to real customer records, and tell people in your privacy policy that data may be processed outside Canada. Public bodies under FIPPA face tighter expectations.

### Can your developers work alongside our in-house team in Vancouver?

Yes, through the Embedded model, where our engineers join your team's routines. Given Pacific hours, that usually means a written daily standup in your chat tool plus one live call a week in the evening overlap window, which is Nepal's morning. It works best when your team already documents decisions in tickets rather than hallway conversations.
