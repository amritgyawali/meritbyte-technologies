## What makes the best web developer in Singapore?

Evidence that they can pass your own due diligence. A developer serving Singapore companies should be able to show all of the following:

1. **PDPA-aware forms.** Purpose stated at the point of collection, marketing consent separate from service consent, and your Data Protection Officer's business contact published.
2. **No unnecessary NRIC collection.** Contact and booking forms do not ask for NRIC numbers, in line with the PDPC's advisory guidelines.
3. **Do Not Call checks** built into any SMS or voice marketing workflow.
4. **PayNow and cards,** through Stripe or a local acquirer, with GST-inclusive consumer pricing.
5. **A security baseline.** OWASP Top 10 covered, multi-factor authentication on admin accounts, dependency updates on a schedule, and honest answers to a vendor security questionnaire.
6. **Hosting in Singapore when you want it.** AWS, Azure and Google Cloud all run Singapore regions.
7. **A language plan.** Which pages need Chinese, Malay or Tamil, and which can stay in English.
8. **Documentation and ownership.** Architecture notes, runbooks, and every repository, domain and cloud account in your company's name.

## What does the PDPA require from your website?

The Personal Data Protection Act 2012 sets obligations for consent, purpose, notification, access and correction, accuracy, protection, retention, transfers abroad, breach notification and accountability. Several land directly on the website.

- **Consent and purpose.** Forms say why data is collected; marketing consent is a separate, unticked choice.
- **A Data Protection Officer.** Every organisation must appoint at least one and make their business contact information available, usually on the privacy page.
- **Breach notification.** Once you assess a breach as notifiable, because it is likely to cause significant harm or affects 500 or more people, you must tell the PDPC within three calendar days.
- **Transfer limitation.** Personal data sent outside Singapore must be protected to a standard comparable with the PDPA, usually by contract. Giving an overseas developer test data rather than live records avoids most of this.
- **Penalties.** Financial penalties can reach 10% of annual Singapore turnover for organisations with turnover above S$10 million, or S$1 million otherwise.

## Which marketing rules apply: Do Not Call and the Spam Control Act?

The Do Not Call provisions in the PDPA require you to check the DNC Registry before sending marketing calls, SMS or faxes to Singapore numbers, unless you hold clear and unambiguous consent in written or other accessible form. A website that collects phone numbers for promotions should record that consent with a timestamp and the wording shown.

The Spam Control Act 2007 governs unsolicited commercial email and SMS sent in bulk. Such messages must carry an `<ADV>` label in the subject line, accurate sender information and a working unsubscribe. The simplest compliance strategy is to send only to people who opted in, which keeps you outside the "unsolicited" category altogether.

## PayNow, cards and GST: how should a Singapore site take payment?

Offer PayNow for local customers and cards for everyone else. Stripe supports both, and local acquirers and wallets such as NETS, GrabPay and Atome fill other gaps depending on your audience.

- GST has been 9% since 1 January 2024, and GST-registered businesses must show consumer prices inclusive of GST.
- PayNow QR codes (SGQR) suit invoices and B2B deposits as well as checkout.
- A .com.sg domain generally requires a Singapore-registered entity with a UEN; plain .sg is more flexible.

If your platform sells regionally, keep SGD as the default and add other currencies deliberately rather than relying on automatic conversion at the card network.

## How should a Singapore website handle English, Chinese, Malay and Tamil?

Build in English first and add other languages where a real audience needs them. Consumer services such as clinics, tuition centres and eldercare often benefit from Chinese pages, and sometimes Malay or Tamil; B2B and regional headquarters sites usually stay in English.

When you do add languages, use separate URLs with hreflang tags such as en-SG, zh-SG, ms-SG and ta-SG, and choose fonts that render Simplified Chinese and Tamil correctly (the Noto families cover both). Tamil in particular needs generous line height. Have each version written by a fluent writer, since machine translation reads poorly to native speakers. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can a vendor outside Singapore comply with the PDPA?

Yes, as long as you set it up properly. Your organisation remains responsible for personal data it shares, so the contract must require protection comparable to the PDPA, and access should be limited to what the work needs. Most website projects avoid transfers entirely by giving developers test data and keeping production access with your own team.

### What hours overlap between Singapore and Nepal?

Nearly the whole working day. Nepal is 2h15m behind Singapore all year, so a 9 a.m. start in Nepal is 11:15 a.m. in Singapore, and Nepal works until 8:15 p.m. Singapore time. Calls can be scheduled from late morning through the afternoon, and evening fixes are often ready for your next morning.

### Can our e-commerce site accept PayNow?

Yes. Stripe supports PayNow for Singapore businesses, and several local acquirers offer it too. At checkout the customer sees a QR code to scan with their banking app, and the order confirms once payment is received. For B2B invoices, a static SGQR code on the invoice is often simpler than an online checkout.

### Should our website be in Chinese as well as English?

Only if a meaningful share of your customers prefer to read Chinese. Many Singapore B2B buyers expect English only, while consumer services for older residents or families often benefit from Chinese pages. Start with your highest-intent pages, have them written by a fluent writer, and measure enquiries before translating the whole site.
